# Audience — ChatSKU

## Primary persona
Owners, sales managers, ecommerce managers at B2B companies in manufacturing, distribution, and wholesale. Annual revenue $1M–$50M, 10–200 employees.

## What they already know
- Their product catalog inside out
- They lose deals to faster competitors
- Phone calls eat their day; after-hours buyers vanish before morning
- AI exists but they're skeptical of generic chatbots
- ERP, CRM, and quoting tools exist but don't solve the front-end buyer problem

## What they don't know or get wrong
- That a catalog assistant can be set up in hours, not months
- That it works without rebuilding the website or migrating data
- That it handles complex B2B pricing (customer groups, RFQs, tiered discounts)
- That it doesn't replace their sales team — it filters and prepares deals
- That their existing catalog files (PDFs, Excel, ERP exports) are enough to get started

## Reading context
Desktop during work hours; sometimes mobile in the evening. 5–10 minutes max. Reading because they have a specific pain: slow quotes, lost after-hours leads, repetitive catalog questions burning sales team time.

## What they hate
- Generic AI hype with no concrete product clarity
- Fake demos that don't match real B2B complexity
- "Schedule a discovery call" gates
- SaaS jargon (orchestration, synergies, ecosystem)
- Oversimplified solutions that assume they have a clean modern tech stack

## Reading level
Industry-fluent for B2B operations. Acronyms OK after first definition (RFQ, ERP, PIM, CSV, CRM, SKU). Don't over-explain manufacturing or distribution basics.

## Geographic note
Existing ChatSKU content targets Dallas/DFW market for local SEO. New content should drop geographic modifiers unless the user explicitly asks for local-SEO targeting.

## Decision authority
These readers have budget authority or influence it directly. They're not researchers — they're evaluating whether to buy. Write to move them toward a decision, not just educate.

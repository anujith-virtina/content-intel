How to add a B2B chatbot to your WooCommerce store

Executive summary

A B2B chatbot for WooCommerce is an AI catalog assistant that reads your full product catalog and answers buyer questions on specs, pricing, and availability around the clock. It works on top of WooCommerce and your B2B plugins, not as a replacement for them.

Most WooCommerce stores bolt on a generic chat widget and see nothing change. The reason is simple. Generic tools answer FAQs. B2B buyers ask catalog questions. Those are different jobs.

This guide covers what a B2B chatbot does on WooCommerce, why B2B stores need one more than B2C, the seven steps to add one, how B2B plugins fit in, real cost ranges, and a worked revenue example.

What is a B2B chatbot for WooCommerce?

A B2B chatbot for WooCommerce is an AI catalog assistant that understands your products, customer-specific pricing, and quote workflows, then answers buyers in plain language 24/7. It is not a support bot, and it is not a marketing pop-up. It sells from your catalog.

The difference comes down to what the tool reads. A support bot reads a help-desk knowledge base. A marketing bot reads canned campaign scripts. A B2B catalog assistant reads your actual WooCommerce catalog: SKUs, variants, custom attributes, stock, and the pricing rules your plugins enforce.

That last part matters. B2B selling runs on customer groups, tiered pricing, minimum order quantities, and RFQ flows. A tool that can't see those answers the wrong question. If you're new to the category, our guide on what a B2B catalog chatbot is covers the fundamentals.

In short: it's the conversational layer that turns a static WooCommerce catalog into something that answers and sells.

Why do WooCommerce B2B stores need a chatbot more than B2C?

WooCommerce B2B stores need a chatbot more than B2C stores because B2B buying is harder to self-serve. The products are technical, the pricing is account-specific, the orders are quote-driven, and the buyers research after hours. A static catalog can't carry that weight. A B2B-aware assistant can.

B2C buying is mostly browse, click, buy. B2B buying is not. Here's why the gap is wider for B2B WooCommerce stores.

  • Complex products. Technical specs, compatibility, and certifications decide the sale. Buyers ask questions a product page can't anticipate.
  • Account-specific pricing. Two buyers see two different prices. Contract tiers and wholesale rates live behind a login, not on the public page.
  • RFQ-driven sales. Many B2B orders never hit add-to-cart. They start as a quote request, then stall in an inbox.
  • Long buying cycles. Multiple stakeholders, multiple visits, weeks of evaluation. A buyer who can't get an answer moves on.
  • After-hours research. Procurement happens at night and on weekends, when your team is offline. We cover this pattern in depth in our breakdown of the after-hours buyer behavior most stores never see.

Gartner found that 67% of B2B buyers prefer a rep-free buying experience for at least part of their purchase (Gartner, 2026). Rep-free only works if something answers when the rep is gone. Without it, you're left with a passive catalog that shows products but can't close.

What can a B2B chatbot actually do on WooCommerce?

A B2B chatbot on WooCommerce answers SKU-level questions, shows customer-specific pricing, builds quotes inside the chat, handles large-catalog discovery, captures after-hours leads, and routes serious buyers to your team with full context. It does the work your product pages and contact form can't.

Here are the six things it does that move revenue.

1. Answer SKU-level product questions

Buyers ask specifics. Voltage, thread size, material grade, lead time. The assistant reads the SKU and answers from your catalog data, not a guess. No ticket, no wait.

2. Show customer-specific pricing

A logged-in buyer asks "what's my price on 100 units?" and gets their tier's number, not list price. The assistant reads the pricing rules your B2B plugin already enforces and explains them in words.

3. Build quotes inside the chat

Instead of a dead-end form, the buyer assembles a quote in conversation. The assistant captures quantities, SKUs, and contact details, then hands a clean RFQ to your team. This is the conversational front end to an automated RFQ workflow.

4. Handle large-catalog discovery

"Show me 1-inch brass valves under $500." A buyer can't filter their way to that on a 4,000-SKU store. The assistant searches the catalog in trade language and returns matches in seconds.

5. Capture after-hours leads

When your team is offline, the assistant keeps selling. It answers, qualifies, and captures the buyer's intent so nothing slips away overnight.

6. Route serious buyers to sales with context

When a buyer is ready, the assistant hands off the full conversation. Your rep opens the deal already knowing the SKUs, the quantity, and the question. No re-discovery. This is an AI sales assistant doing the qualifying before a human ever picks up.

How do you add a B2B chatbot to your WooCommerce store?

To add a B2B chatbot to your WooCommerce store, export your catalog, choose a B2B-specific platform, connect your customer groups and pricing, configure your RFQ workflow, add the script to your theme, test with a real buyer scenario, then go live. Most of the work is configuration, not code.

Here are the seven steps, in order.

  1. Export your WooCommerce product catalog. Use WooCommerce's built-in product CSV exporter to pull SKUs, attributes, and variations. A direct WooCommerce connection or REST sync works too.
  2. Choose a B2B-specific platform. Pick a tool built to read a catalog and B2B pricing, not a generic support widget. This single choice decides whether the project works. Closely related is B2B conversational commerce as a strategy.
  3. Connect your customer groups and pricing tiers. Map your wholesale roles, contract tiers, and account-level prices so the assistant answers each buyer with the right number.
  4. Configure your RFQ workflow. If you sell quote-first, define how the assistant captures line items and routes the request to your team.
  5. Add the script to your WooCommerce theme. Paste one snippet into your theme, or drop it in with a plugin. No rebuild, no developer sprint.
  6. Test with a real B2B scenario. Pretend you're a buyer. Ask for a tiered price, request a quote, search for an obscure SKU. Fix the gaps before buyers find them.
  7. Go live. Turn it on. Watch the after-hours conversations start landing in your inbox the same week.

What about WooCommerce B2B plugins? Do you still need a chatbot?

Yes, you still need a chatbot. WooCommerce B2B plugins structure your data. They don't talk to buyers. A plugin sets the tiered price. The assistant explains that price to a buyer asking a question at 9pm. They solve different halves of the same problem, and they work together.

The B2B plugin space is mature. The main players each handle pricing, customer groups, and quotes in their own way.

  • B2BKing. A full dynamic rule engine, customer groups, unique per-customer prices, and a built-in RFQ module.
  • Wholesale Suite. Wholesale roles and price fields per product. Simpler to set up, lighter on quote logic.
  • B2B for WooCommerce. Customer groups, tiered pricing, and request-a-quote flows in one extension.

These plugins are the plumbing. They decide what a buyer is allowed to see and pay. What they don't do is answer the buyer who's confused, comparing, or shopping after hours. That's the gap a B2B catalog assistant fills. Example: B2BKing sets the 100-unit tier price. The assistant answers "what's my price on 100 units, and what's the lead time?" in the buyer's own words.

So the rule is simple. Keep your plugin. Add the conversational layer on top. If you sell through distribution, our page built for distributors and wholesalers shows how the two fit together.

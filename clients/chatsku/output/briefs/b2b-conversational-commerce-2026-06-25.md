## Thesis / original POV (the gap)
Every top-ranking "conversational commerce" article is anchored in B2C retail (WhatsApp DMs, Instagram shopping, Sephora bots). **Nobody owns the B2B definition.** Our angle: conversational commerce is a B2B *operations layer*, not a retail chat widget. It is defined by the four things B2C tools and generic chatbots cannot do: handle RFQ workflows, deliver contract-tier pricing at the moment of inquiry, navigate large-SKU catalogs in trade language, and capture after-hours leads with full deal context.

## Case snippet (before/after, illustrative) — section 6
Representative $8M industrial distributor. 60,000 SKUs. ~200 wholesale accounts on custom contract pricing. ~120 after-hours catalog inquiries/month.
- **Before:** 9pm RFQ lands. List price on the page does not match the buyer's contract rate. Search does not recognize the part number. Buyer leaves; the lead is never logged. Reps spend mornings answering "what's my price / is it in stock."
- **After:** the assistant applies the account's contract tier, confirms stock, generates the quote, and routes it with full context. Capturing even 30% of those 120 inquiries = ~36 extra qualified leads/month; at a ~$4,200 average order and an 18% close rate, that is roughly **$27K/month** recovered. (Label explicitly as an illustrative scenario, not a guaranteed result.)

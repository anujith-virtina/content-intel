## Thesis

> AI takes the roughly 72% of a rep's week that was never really selling, not the rep, and whether that turns into a stronger team or a stalled rollout depends entirely on what the manager does with the hours it frees up.

## Why this, why now, why us

- **Why this angle:** the FAQ answer "no, it doesn't replace your sales team" appears nine times across the corpus and is never explained. This article is the explanation, aimed at the exact reader (sales manager, 1-8 rep team) who is under-served everywhere else — two mentions of "sales manager" in 38 posts.
- **Why now:** the AI-jobs conversation is loud and mostly aimed at SaaS SDR pods. Nobody has written the small-distributor version. The gap is open right now and easy to occupy with genuinely concrete, honest content.

## Audience

Owners and sales managers at B2B manufacturers, distributors and wholesalers, $1M-$50M revenue, 1-8 person inside sales teams. They are skeptical of AI hype and specifically worried about two things this article must answer directly: will this cost my best rep their job, and will a new hire lean on the tool instead of learning the catalog. 5-10 minute read, desktop during work hours.

### H2: What goes wrong when the rollout is careless? (~250 words, mandatory section)

Frame all four as consistent practitioner patterns, not statistics. Say plainly that research could not tie them to one named study.

- Reps quietly route around a tool they don't trust. Trust breaks the first time it gives a wrong stock number or a stale price, and it is slow to rebuild.
- Nobody owns the handoff. A captured lead dies exactly as fast as it would in a shared inbox nobody checks, if no one is explicitly responsible for picking it up.
- The manager doesn't change what they measure. If "calls made" stays the scoreboard after routine questions move to the assistant, the freed hours get eaten by busywork instead of relationship work (this is where the earlier plant from the manager section pays off).
- A new rep leans on the assistant instead of learning the catalog. Flag as a reasoned risk worth watching, not a documented finding.
- Internal link: anchor "the response gap" → `/what-is-the-response-gap/`, placed naturally next to the "nobody owns the handoff" point.

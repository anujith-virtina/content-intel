# Brief: What is agentic commerce? Explained for non-technical teams

## 1. Thesis

> Agentic commerce means the thing on the other end of your product inquiry is increasingly software, and software that cannot find your answer does not call you back, it just leaves you off the list.

The honest core, in the words the creator should keep in view the whole time: **the buying has not been automated. The choosing has.**

This is not a "robots will buy from you" post. Truly autonomous purchasing is still rare. What already changed is that the shortlist gets drawn before anyone contacts you, by something that will never ask a follow-up question.

## 5. Structure

Total target **1,600-1,750 words** of body prose. Hard range 1,400-1,800. Structural labels stay verbatim per rules section 2. All five body H2s are questions answered in the first sentence.

### Opening hook (inside Introduction)

A spec question arrives at 2pm on an ordinary Tuesday. Not a phone call, not an email, not after hours. Something reads three pages of the site, does not find whether the part ships this week, and leaves. Nobody at the company ever knows it happened. **The hook must land the "silent loss in daylight" idea.** This is deliberately not the 8pm buyer, not the after-hours post, and the creator must not reach for "night-shift sales rep" anywhere in this piece.

### H2: Executive summary — 120 words

Verbatim label. Three short paragraphs. Must state the thesis plainly (the buying has not been automated, the choosing has), name the one thing that changes (software does not call back), and preview the four questions the post answers. **No ChatSKU mention here.** Education-first, per the 2044 and 2684 precedent.

### H2: Introduction — 160 words

The Tuesday scenario, then the one-sentence definition, then the promise: no acronyms, no platform project, no assumption you have an IT department.

### H2 (B1): What is agentic commerce, in plain language? — 190 words

- **Direct answer, first sentence.** Defined by who is on the other end of the inquiry, not by what the phrase means. Business consequence inside two sentences.
- **Anchor analogy goes here: "you have been selling to software since 1979."** Electronic purchase orders replaced phoned and faxed orders decades ago; any distributor over a certain size already has customers whose systems send orders without a human typing them. Agentic commerce is that, minus the eighteen-month onboarding project and the agreement signed in advance. The old way: you and your customer agreed on a format and connected the pipes. The new way: the buyer's software just shows up and tries to read you, with no agreement and no warning. **Say "electronic purchase orders." Never "EDI," never a transaction-set number.**
- Close with the single link to `/agentic-commerce-glossary/`.
- **Do not include:** any protocol name, any category-style definition, any comparison of conversational versus agentic beyond half a sentence.

### H2 (B2): Does this replace your sales reps? — 210 words

- **Direct answer, first sentence: no, and the data leans the other way.**
- Gartner's May 2026 survey: 69% of B2B buyers go to a sales rep to check what the AI told them.
- Gartner separately predicts that by 2030, 75% of B2B buyers will prefer sales experiences that put human interaction first.
- **The honest tension is the section's payoff, and it must be stated out loud:** the same firm forecasting that most B2B buying will run through AI also forecasts buyers swinging back toward people. Nobody knows which wins. What is not in dispute is what the software takes first.
- The role change, said plainly: software takes over the part of the day that was never selling anyway, the stock checks, the price confirmations, the spec lookups. The rep becomes the person the buyer calls to check the software's answer.
- **Never say "replaces your sales team" or any near-paraphrase** (brand.md).

### H2 (B3): Are your customers actually doing this yet? — 200 words

- **Direct answer: more than you think, and more than you think they think.**
- Deloitte: 40% of B2B buyers already use agentic AI in purchasing, against 24% of suppliers.
- Deloitte: 72% of suppliers describe their sales process as mostly or highly automated. Only 47% of buyers agree. This is the emotional centre of the post. Give it room.
- Forrester expects one in five B2B sellers to be pushed into agent-led quote negotiation during 2026, which is the answer to "is this a 2030 problem."
- **Required honesty note, in this section, in plain words:** nobody has measured this in industrial distribution specifically. The available research covers software buyers, enterprise procurement, and retail. Say it out loud and say we are not going to pretend otherwise. This matches the precedent set by posts 2665 and 2783 and is the reason the reader should trust the rest.

### H2 (B4): If software only sees your price, what happens to your margin? — 250 words

- **Direct answer: you lose on price, because price is the only thing you gave it to compare.**
- **The load board analogy carries this section.** A carrier does not get picked because the dispatcher likes them. They get picked because their rate, their lane, and their availability were legible on a screen at the moment the load posted. Agentic commerce is a load board being built around your product catalog.
- The sharpest mechanism in the research, in plain language: when the buyer's software and the seller's software negotiate, both walk straight down to the seller's floor price. Nobody in that exchange has a reason to stop.
- The counter, and the practical heart of the post: availability, lead time, contract terms, and cross-reference accuracy have to be as visible as price. If the only fact about you that is easy to find is a number, that number is your whole pitch.
- Link `/ai-ready-b2b-catalog-autonomous-buying/` once here, anchor "AI-ready product catalog". Do not rebuild its argument.
- **Do not include:** the Kearney 500 basis point figure, any basis-point language, any invented dollar amount, any ROI model. See section 6.

### H2 (B5): Do you need to hire a developer to deal with this? — 200 words

- **Direct answer: not to start. The first moves are business decisions, not engineering ones.**
- Four moves, in plain trade language: decide what you are actually willing to publish; get stock and lead-time answers into a place software can reach; make sure your own product information says what your reps say on the phone; stop hiding the facts a buyer needs in order to choose you.
- ChatSKU appears here and only here in the body, education-first and bounded: it reads the catalog files you already have, a PDF, a spreadsheet, an ERP extract, and answers buyer questions from them. **No claim of an automatic live pull from any named ERP** (the post-1455 correction). One line of code is fine to say. Do not oversell.
- Links: `/erp-export-ai-agent-ready/` (anchor "ERP export self-check") for the technical person on the team, `/for-b2b-manufacturers-distributors-and-wholesalers/` (anchor "manufacturers and distributors"), `/features/` (anchor "what ChatSKU connects to").

### H2: People Also Ask — 150 words, 4 H3 questions

1. **Is agentic commerce the same as a chatbot on a website?** No. A chat window waits to be asked. Agentic software goes looking and decides. One sentence on the distinction, no section, no link (fence E).
2. **Which orders will software buy first?** The routine third of the order book: commodity items, small repeat spend, straight reorders. Not the eleven-year account with redlined terms. Reasoning only, no statistic.
3. **Can a small distributor compete when software is choosing?** Yes, and being small may be the opening. Software carries no incumbent bias. It does not know who is bigger, only what it can read. **Mechanism only, no G2 figure.**
4. **Is anyone in industrial distribution actually doing this yet?** Honest answer: not in a confirmed, published way. Amazon Business has shipped buyer-facing AI tools, so the direction is not theoretical, but nobody has a verified agentic purchasing implementation in this trade. Preserve post 2129's honesty on this without reusing its wording.

### H2: Conclusion — 100 words

Verbatim label. Dark navy section, white centred heading, `#aaaacc` centred body. **No inline links in the body copy.** Restate the thesis in one line. Give one instruction the reader can act on this week: ask what your own website can tell a stranger about stock, lead time, and price without a phone call. Then the `#e94560` button to `/demo/`, "See the live demo."

### H2: Frequently asked questions — 180 words, 5 H3 questions, native accordion

1. What does agentic commerce mean in one sentence?
2. Will an AI agent negotiate my prices down?
3. Do I have to publish my pricing publicly to be included?
4. What if the software gets my product specs wrong? (One or two sentences. No link, no expansion. Fence A boundary 5.)
5. What is the first thing I should do about this in the next ninety days?

FAQ answers must not repeat body sentences verbatim. **The credit-bureau analogy belongs in FAQ 2 or 3:** you already let software make a consequential commercial judgement about your customers, and you do not personally re-verify every credit report. The unfamiliar part is not software making decisions, it is software making them about you, using facts you did not choose.

## The honest research finding

**No credible industry-segmented B2B response-time benchmark exists.** Searches returned aggregator SEO
content only (aloware, greetnow, limecall, optifai, artemisgtm, bowenaistrategygroup, goautonomous). Their
numbers ("manufacturing takes three days", "47-hour average", "32% close rate under 5 minutes") are stated
without traceable methodology and frequently contradict each other. **All rejected.**

Consequence: this post does **not** publish a per-industry response-time table. It makes a **structural**
argument, which must be stated plainly in body text: the industries differ in what the buyer is waiting
*for*, not in a measured response-time figure we can source.

## Verified sources (2 externals, at the cap)

### 1. ECIA, "Understanding Semiconductor Lead Times" — electronics section
https://www.ecianow.org/2026/06/25/semiconductorleadtimes/ (page 200; figures read from ECIA's own PDF)

- Complex wafer fabrication takes **up to 12 weeks**
- Assembly and test add **another 4 to 8 weeks**
- Full semiconductor cycle **approximately 26 weeks**
- Raw materials to chips: **16 to 26 weeks**
- Distributors hold roughly **180 days of inventory (~26 weeks)**; OEM/CEM about 65 days (~9 weeks)
- A new foundry costs **$10 to $20 billion** to build
- **"Customers double order when supply tightens"**, creating a bullwhip through the channel

Use: the electronics buyer's clock is set by a 26-week cycle they cannot influence. Availability answers
decay. That is why a three-day reply is not merely slow, it can be stale.

### 2. Senseye / Siemens, "The True Cost of Downtime 2024" — industrial supply section
https://assets.new.siemens.com/siemens/assets/api/uuid:1b43afb5-2d07-47f7-9eb7-893fe7d0bc59/TCOD-2024_original.pdf (200)

- Fortune Global 500 lose **almost $1.4 trillion annually** to unplanned downtime, **equivalent to 11% of
  annual revenues**
- An hour of downtime in a large **automotive** plant now costs **$2.3 million**, more than $600 a second
- At the low end, a lost hour costs **$36,000 in Fast Moving Consumer Goods**
- An average large plant loses **27 hours a month** to unplanned downtime, down from 39 in 2019
- Roughly **326 hours a year** per plant
- SME downtime reaches **$150,000 an hour** at the top end

**Framing discipline:** these are the *buyer's* losses while their line is stopped, not distributor
response times. Say so explicitly. Misrepresenting it as a response-time statistic would be dishonest and
the post's whole credibility rests on the distinction.

## The thesis

The response gap is not one number. Each industry runs on a **different clock**, so "respond faster" means
something different in each, and the cost of the gap surfaces somewhere different.

- **Hardware and fasteners: the breadth clock.** Thousands of near-identical SKUs, low unit value, requests
  arriving as multi-line lists. The economics of quoting a 40-line list by hand are terrible, so reps
  rationally deprioritise exactly the requests that are easiest to win. The gap costs you volume, quietly.
- **Electronics components: the availability clock.** The answer the buyer needs (do you have it, what is
  the alternate, when does it land) changes while the request sits. Delay does not just annoy, it makes
  your eventual answer wrong.
- **Industrial supply and MRO: the downtime clock.** The buyer is not shopping. A line is stopped and every
  hour is metered. A slow reply does not lose a quote, it loses the account.

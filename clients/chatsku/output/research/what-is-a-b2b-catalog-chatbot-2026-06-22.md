## Key factual content by section

**What is it (definition):** A B2B catalog chatbot is an AI assistant that ingests a company's own product catalog (PDF, Excel, HTML, or ERP/PIM data), understands buyer questions in natural language, and answers product, spec, availability, and pricing questions in a chat interface, then captures RFQs/leads. It reads YOUR catalog, not the internet (RAG — retrieval augmented generation, explained plainly).

**Why B2B catalogs specifically need it (the core argument):**
- SKU complexity: industrial distributors carry 50,000–500,000+ SKUs
- Multi-layer pricing: negotiated contracts, volume discounts, customer tiers, MOQs
- Trade-language search: buyers using their own terms get nothing on standard search
- Contract vs list price mismatch: buyers see list prices not matching negotiated rates → abandon self-service, call to confirm (most concrete, relatable pain point)
- RFQ/quote workflows, compatibility/spec lookups, ERP-sourced data
- After-hours buyers: 40%+ browse evenings, 24% weekends, when reps are offline

**How it works (plain language):** ingests/indexes catalog + ERP/PIM data → interprets buyer intent in natural language → retrieves accurate SKU-level answers (RAG: pulls from your indexed data first, not general AI knowledge) → applies pricing/availability rules → captures RFQ/lead and routes to sales. Buyer doesn't need to know "RAG"; they need "it reads your catalog, not the internet."

**Who it's for / use cases:** distributors with large SKU counts; manufacturers with spec-heavy products; wholesalers with tiered/contract pricing; teams losing after-hours inquiries; teams where reps spend 15–20 min per availability+price lookup.

**What to look for (BUYER EDUCATION framing — NOT post 294's scorecard):** Frame as "what should this thing actually be able to do, and why does each matter to a buyer like you" — accuracy on YOUR data, handling your pricing logic, working from whatever catalog format you already have, answering when staff are offline, handing off to a human cleanly. Keep it conceptual, not a vendor checklist. Then point to post 294 to compare specific tools.

**How it differs from a generic chatbot (reframed for buyer education):** A generic/support chatbot follows scripted FAQ flows about policies and orders. A catalog chatbot must reason over a live, structured product catalog with thousands of SKUs and customer-specific pricing. Rephrase post 294's contrast; do not reproduce the "part 4471-B" line verbatim. Frame around what the BUYER experiences, not a feature matrix.

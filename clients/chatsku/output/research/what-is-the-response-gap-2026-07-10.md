## 5. Recommended unique angle

**Angle:** "What is the response gap?" is the single-topic, definitional companion to the `/response-gap/` problem page. It should:
1. Define the response gap plainly and early: the delay between a buyer's first inquiry (RFQ, spec question, contact form) and a human sales reply — reframed, in the page's own words, as "not a staffing problem, a timing problem."
2. Name its two concrete failure moments, mirroring the page's own cost metrics: (a) RFQ/quote-request delay (3-day average without automation) and (b) after-hours inquiry silence ($0 revenue captured after hours, 1-hour window before buyers move to a competitor).
3. Quantify the gap independently using the HBR "Short Life of Online Sales Leads" stats (42-hour average response, 23% never respond, 1-hour responders ~7x more likely to qualify) as the primary backbone — distinct sourcing from the MIT 5-minute stat already used in posts 397/299.
4. Show the fix as fast and structural, echoing "One deployment. Problem closed." — the overnight, one-line-of-code deploy promise already established in ChatSKU's brand voice ("night-shift sales rep").
5. Explicitly is NOT: a switching-supplier competitive-loss article (that's `b2b-customers-leave-for-faster-competitors`), not a post-quote follow-up article (that's post 299), not a broad after-hours ROI narrative (that's post 186), not a search/discovery-layer argument (that's `funnel-inversion-answer-first`).

**Recommended format:** Format B (Conversational Q&A) + definitional/story-hook pattern, matching post 397's proven structure exactly (question-style H2s, PAA block, FAQ accordion, one worked ROI example, one comparison table contrasting "with a response gap" vs "response gap closed"). This keeps structural variety reasonable (Format B is now used often, but it is the correct format for a definitional companion piece per the format-selection guidance, and post 397 is the explicit structural model the user asked to follow).

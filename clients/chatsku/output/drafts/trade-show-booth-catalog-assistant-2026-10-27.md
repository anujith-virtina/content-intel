# Your trade show booth closes at 5 pm. Your catalog can keep working.

## Executive summary

Trade shows pack a year of buyer attention into two or three days. The serious conversations happen at the booth, but a large share of the real questions come later. A facilities manager walks the floor at 4 pm, wants to know whether a fitting comes in a 316 stainless version with a 50-piece minimum, and leaves with a business card. The rep sends a follow-up email the next morning. By then the buyer has already asked the same question at the booth next door.

This guide explains where a catalog assistant fits into trade show follow-up. It covers which booth questions it can answer from your catalog files, which ones still need a person, how to prepare the catalog before the show, and what to do with the questions it cannot answer. It also covers how to tell whether the show produced more qualified conversations than a pile of business cards did.

The plan works for a manufacturer or distributor with a catalog in PDF, Excel, or an ERP export, and a website that can carry one line of code. It does not require a developer on staff or a rebuilt site.

## Introduction: the question that arrives after the booth closes

Consider an illustrative case: a mid-size industrial supplier at a regional maintenance show. The booth is busy from opening to close. Between conversations, the rep answers questions from memory, pulls a binder of spec sheets, and promises to send the rest. The catalog has several thousand SKUs spread across a PDF, two spreadsheets, and an ERP export that only the operations team reads.

On the second afternoon, a buyer scans the QR code on the booth sign and types a question into the catalog page on the company website. It is 4:40 pm. The rep is packing up. The buyer wants stock availability for a specific size, a finish, and the price for an account that buys in volume.

Without a catalog assistant, that question waits until morning. With one, the buyer gets an answer from the catalog files, or a clear note that a person will follow up, while the interest is still fresh. Many trade show teams have no way to answer a question at 4:40 pm on a Tuesday. The rest of this guide is about building one.

## Who uses a trade show catalog assistant?

Three people touch the process, and each one needs something different from it.

The booth rep needs fewer repeated questions during the show. The [AI sales assistant for B2B ecommerce](https://chatsku.com/ai-sales-assistant-b2b-ecommerce/) page describes the same question-answering flow from the buyer's side. A rep who spends the day answering "do you have this in stainless?" cannot hold a useful conversation with the buyer who needs a custom specification. The assistant takes the routine questions so the rep can spend the day on the conversations that need judgment.

The sales manager needs a clean list of who asked what, and which questions went unanswered. Those unanswered questions are the most valuable output of the whole show, because they show where the catalog, the pricing rules, or the product range falls short of what buyers actually want.

The owner or marketing lead needs to know whether the show was worth the cost. That answer depends on qualified conversations and their follow-up speed, not on how many people scanned the badge.

## Which booth questions can a catalog assistant answer on its own?

The honest answer is that it depends on what is in your catalog files. A catalog assistant answers from the catalog it has been given, so the quality of the answer depends on the quality of the data. ChatSKU reads the files you already have, including PDF price lists, Excel sheets, ERP exports, and CSV files, and builds the assistant from them.

### Questions the catalog files can settle

Some questions are factual and sit in the catalog. These include stock or availability for a listed item, the size, material, or finish options, the minimum order quantity, the case or pack size, and the lead time when it is written in the file. A buyer who asks these at 4:40 pm should get the answer immediately.

### Questions that still need a person

Other questions depend on judgment or on information outside the catalog. Custom engineering, a special finish that is not in the file, a site visit, and negotiated contract pricing for a large account all belong to a person. A good assistant does not guess at these. It recognizes that the question is outside its catalog and passes it on with the buyer's details attached.

### Questions that depend on who is asking

Pricing is the tricky category. If you sell with customer groups or tiered pricing, the price a buyer sees may depend on the account they belong to. Decide before the show which prices a visitor can see without signing in, which ones need an account, and which ones always come from a rep. Write those rules down, then check that the assistant follows them. A booth visitor who sees the wrong price has a worse experience than one who sees no price at all.

## How do you prepare the catalog before the show?

Preparation matters more than the setup. The one-line script tag takes minutes to add to a page. Cleaning up the catalog takes weeks, and it is where most of the value comes from.

### Start with the SKUs that drive booth questions

Look back at the last two shows, or at the last few months of inbound questions. Pick the products that buyers asked about most, and keep the list short enough that you can finish it before the show. Make sure those products have complete, consistent entries: size, material, finish, pack size, minimum order, and lead time. An assistant can only answer confidently about what it can read clearly.

### Reconcile the PDF with the spreadsheet

Trade show catalogs are often built from a PDF that was last updated six months ago, while the spreadsheet has changed twice since then. Before the show, check the two against each other for the products you plan to feature. When they disagree, the assistant will reflect whichever file it reads, so pick the current one and retire the old one. The same check applies to ERP exports, which sometimes hold a unit of measure the printed catalog does not.

### Decide what the booth sign links to

The QR code or short link on the booth sign should go to a page that already carries the assistant, not to a generic homepage. This is a small detail that changes how the show performs. The visitor lands on the page where the questions can be answered, and the rep does not have to explain the next step.

## What should happen to a question the assistant cannot answer?

This is where most trade show plans fall apart. The assistant answers the easy questions well. The hard ones either disappear or get routed to a shared inbox where they wait for two days.

### Turn each open question into a lead

When the assistant cannot answer, it should capture the question, the product, the buyer's contact details, and the time. ChatSKU builds quote requests from the same catalog conversation, so a buyer who asks for a volume price can move into a request for quote without repeating what they already said. The request then goes into your existing workflow.

### Send the lead into the system your team already uses

A lead from a trade show should land where the rest of your leads land. ChatSKU connects to CRM and ecommerce systems (see the [feature overview](https://chatsku.com/features/)), so the captured questions can be sent into the tools your sales team already uses, instead of a separate spreadsheet that only one person checks. Confirm the specific connection with your implementation contact before the show, because the exact fields and the sync schedule depend on your setup.

For a closer look at why speed matters once a quote is requested, the [RFQ form best practices](https://chatsku.com/rfq-form-best-practices/) guide covers what a quote request needs to include, and the [quote-to-order automation](https://chatsku.com/b2b-quote-to-order-automation/) post explains what happens when the reply is slow.

### Set a response time before the show

Decide how quickly each open question gets a human reply, for example by the next business morning, and assign one person to own that queue for the duration of the show. Without an owner, the captured questions are only a list. With one, they become the follow-up plan.

## How do you measure whether the show was worth it?

Business card counts and scan totals tell you very little. A better measure is the number of conversations that reached a specific next step, such as a quote request, a sample request, or a scheduled call, and how long each one took to get a reply.

### Count qualified conversations, not contacts

Separate the buyers who asked a specific question from the people who only picked up a brochure. Specific questions, such as a question about a finish or a volume price, are a stronger signal than a general interest in the company. Track those separately in the report you send after the show.

### Track the questions the catalog could not answer

The unanswered questions are a free list of catalog gaps. If buyers keep asking for a size you do not list, or for a finish that exists only in a spec sheet, the catalog needs an update. Bring that list to the product team after the show. It will tell you more about what to add to the next catalog than any feedback form.

### Compare follow-up speed with last year

If you attended the show before, compare the time between a question and a reply. A catalog assistant changes that number for the questions it can answer, and the queue process changes it for the rest. The difference is usually more useful than the total lead count.

## People also ask

### Does a trade show catalog assistant replace the booth rep?

No. It answers routine, factual questions from the catalog, which frees the rep for conversations that need judgment, negotiation, or a relationship. The rep still closes the important deals.

### Do I need a tablet or a kiosk at the booth?

Not necessarily. The buyer can use their own phone by scanning the code on the booth sign, which opens the catalog page on your website. A tablet at the booth is a useful extra for demonstrations, but it is not required for the assistant to work.

### Can I set this up in the weeks before a show?

The technical setup is quick, but the catalog cleanup is not. The [PDF catalog conversion guide](https://chatsku.com/convert-pdf-catalog-to-website/) covers how a static catalog turns into searchable data. Start the data work at least a few weeks before the show so the assistant reads current, consistent files. Leave time to test the questions buyers actually ask.

### What should I tell buyers who ask something after the show?

Tell them when they will hear back and who will reply. A clear timeline is more useful than a fast reply to the wrong question, and it keeps the buyer from assuming the question was lost.

## Conclusion: make the show's questions count after the lights go off

A trade show concentrates buyer attention, and then the attention fades. The questions that come in after the booth closes are often the most specific ones, because the buyer has already seen the product and is comparing options. A catalog assistant can answer the factual ones immediately, capture the rest with the buyer's details, and send them into the process your team already runs.

The work that matters most happens before the show. Clean the files for the products buyers ask about, reconcile the PDF with the spreadsheet, and decide what a visitor can see without signing in. Then assign one person to own the open-question queue, and agree on a reply time before the first day.

## Frequently asked questions

### How quickly can a trade show page get a catalog assistant?

The technical step is one line of code on the page the booth sign links to. The longer task is preparing the catalog files so the answers are accurate, which is worth doing before the show rather than during it.

### Which catalog formats can the assistant read?

ChatSKU builds the assistant from catalog files in common formats, including PDF price lists, Excel spreadsheets, ERP exports, and CSV files. The cleaner and more consistent the file, the more reliable the answers.

### What happens to a question the assistant cannot answer?

The assistant captures the question with the product, the buyer's contact details, and the time, then passes it on for a person to answer. Set a reply time and an owner before the show so the question does not wait in a shared inbox.

### Can the assistant show different prices to different buyers?

It can, if your pricing uses customer groups or tiers. Decide which prices are visible without an account, which need one, and which always come from a rep. Test the rules before the show so the right price reaches the right buyer.

### Does a catalog assistant replace the sales team at a trade show?

No. It handles routine, factual questions so the team can spend more time on conversations that need judgment. The reps still negotiate, recommend custom configurations, and close the important deals.

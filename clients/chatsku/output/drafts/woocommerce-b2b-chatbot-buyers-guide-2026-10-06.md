Neither was written for a WooCommerce store running a B2B pricing plugin, so neither asks the questions that actually separate a B2B-aware tool from a relabeled generic one.</p>

This isn't a ranking, and it isn't a pitch for <a href="/ai-sales-assistant-b2b-ecommerce/">a B2B AI sales assistant</a> over any other category of tool. <h2>Does the vendor's demo ever touch your actual pricing plugin?</h2>
<p>Usually not, and that's the first thing worth testing when you're comparing a woocommerce b2b chatbot. Generic "best WooCommerce chatbot" checklists score a tool on native WooCommerce data access, setup time, and whether the vendor's own pricing is clear. None of that confirms whether the bot reads B2BKing, Wholesale Suite, WholesaleX, or Addify, because none of those checklists mention a B2B pricing plugin at all.</p>
<p>That gap matters more than any feature list. A chatbot that reads your public catalog but not your pricing plugin will happily quote a logged-out visitor's list price to your biggest wholesale account. It looks like it works in a demo built around the vendor's own test store. That tells you nothing about yours, especially if you're running the kind of catalog a <a href="/for-b2b-manufacturers-distributors-and-wholesalers/">manufacturer, distributor, or wholesaler</a> typically manages.</p>
<p>The actual test: ask the vendor to run the demo on your store, logged in as a real wholesale account, with your pricing plugin active. Not their sandbox. Yours. If the vendor demos with list prices only, or doesn't recognize your plugin's name when you say it, that's your answer before you've asked a second question.</p>

<h2>Is the B2B evaluation checklist you found actually built for WooCommerce?</h2>
<p>Probably not. Most of the B2B evaluation frameworks circulating online were written with an ERP in the loop, something with years of integration history and a dedicated IT budget behind it. The questions they tell you to ask are about integration depth with systems a WooCommerce store usually doesn't run at all.</p>
<p>That mismatch doesn't just waste your time, it points you at the wrong follow-up question. "Which ERP do you integrate with" isn't useful if you don't have one. The question that actually tells you something is: which of my store's plugins do you read, and how do you read it. A vendor who answers in generic terms, "we connect to WooCommerce," without naming your specific pricing plugin, hasn't actually been tested against your stack.</p>
<p>You don't need the engineering answer to that question in a demo call, just confirmation it's been tested against something real. The full technical breakdown of how a woocommerce b2b chatbot reads plugin-layer pricing data lives in a <a href="/woocommerce-b2b-chatbot-integration/">separate integration guide</a>, written for the reader who's already picked a vendor and wants the architecture. This one is for the reader who hasn't picked yet.</p>

<h2>Does "handles variations" mean your variations, or their demo product?</h2>
<p>Ask which one before you believe the claim. WooCommerce gives you simple, variable, and grouped products, and a B2B store stacks customer-group pricing on top of all three. A checklist that just confirms a tool "reads variations" is checking the easy case, a single clean variable product with one price. It's not checking what your store actually runs.</p>
<p>The gap shows up fast once you load something real. Pick your messiest variable product, the one with a dozen size and material options and a tiered price that changes by customer group, and watch what the bot quotes a buyer logged into that specific group. A tool that passed every item on someone's "handles variations" checklist can still hand that buyer the public list price, because the checklist never tested the combination of variation and tier together.</p>
<p>Run this one live, in the demo, with your own product, not theirs. It takes five minutes and it tells you more than the whole checklist did.</p>

<h2>What happens to the bill in your highest-volume month?</h2>
<p>Ask before you sign, not after the invoice. A flat monthly rate sounds simple until you find the fine print on message or conversation caps. If a vendor charges per conversation past a threshold, a bill that looked reasonable in a quiet month can swing hard the month your catalog gets a seasonal spike in traffic.</p>
<p>None of the evaluation guides built for this category raise that question upfront, and plugin pricing pages aren't much help either. Plugin and chatbot pricing both change often enough, and vary enough by plan and add-on, that quoting a specific number here would be stale the day it published. Check current pricing directly with whichever vendor you're evaluating, and ask the question that actually protects you: what's the overage model, and what would last month's real traffic have cost under it.</p>
<p>Push for the worst-case number, not the advertised floor. If you want to model what a volume spike would cost under a few different pricing structures before you're on a call with a salesperson, <a href="/roi-calculator/">running the numbers yourself</a> first gives you a baseline to compare any vendor's answer against.</p>

<h2>What does the bot do when it doesn't know the answer?</h2>
<p>Ask this one directly, and listen for a specific answer. The riskiest unknown in any WooCommerce-scale catalog isn't what the bot gets right. It's what it does when it's uncertain about a price, a spec, or whether an item's in stock. Does it guess, does it escalate to a person, or does it say so and stop.</p>
<p>This is a real test you can run in the demo itself, not a hypothetical. Ask a question the bot genuinely shouldn't be able to answer confidently, something outside the catalog data you've loaded, and watch what happens. A vendor who can describe their escalation behavior in one concrete sentence has clearly thought about it. A vendor who talks around the question, or claims the bot "always gets it right," hasn't, and that's the one most likely to hand a buyer a confident wrong answer.</p>
<p>Guessing on a spec question costs you a returned order. Guessing on a price costs you a buyer's trust the first time the invoice doesn't match what the bot quoted.</p>

<h2>If you switch pricing plugins next year, does the chatbot still work?</h2>
<p>Ask where the setup actually lives before you commit to it. Some integrations connect at the catalog-data layer, reading whatever pricing plugin is active. Others get hard-wired to one plugin's specific structure. You won't feel the difference on day one. You'll feel it the day you migrate from, say, Wholesale Suite to B2BKing and discover the chatbot's pricing logic broke along with it.</p>
<p>The actual question to ask: what does data export and plugin migration look like, and does switching plugins require a new deployment or just a config change. A vendor who can answer that specifically, rather than assuring you it'll "just work," is telling you something real about how their product is built.</p>

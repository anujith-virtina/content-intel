## Introduction

Two buyers search for an "AI product tool" in the same week. This is a scene, not a case study.

The first runs a distributor with a 3,000-line PDF price book. Her buyers ask which size fits and what the price is at 200 units.

Both searches land on the same shortlist. The two need very different tools. This page sorts out which is which.

## What are ChatSKU and Zoovu?

ChatSKU answers buyers from the catalog files you already have. Zoovu runs discovery, configuration and quoting on top of structured product data. They overlap, but they are not the same size of product.

Zoovu (zoovu.com) is an AI product discovery platform for B2C and B2B companies, with modules for AI search, guided selling, visual CPQ and self-service RFQ. Its pages also list a product advisor called Zoe and a data enrichment and catalog management group (checked 2026-11-05).

ChatSKU is the AI catalog assistant at [chatsku.com](https://chatsku.com/). It reads your catalog, answers buyer questions in plain language, and takes quote requests in chat. It has one product, not a suite.

Where do the two overlap? Both answer questions in natural language. Both can take a quote or RFQ request from a buyer. Both list customer-specific pricing, multi-language support, lead capture and analytics.

Both can also sit beside an ERP or PIM. The difference is how much work each one expects you to do first.

## Are you solving finding and asking, or configuring and quoting?

Finding and asking means a buyer needs an answer from a catalog. Configuring and quoting means a buyer must build a valid combination before a quote means anything. Which one you have should decide the tool.

Finding and asking sounds like this: "Do you stock this in stainless?" or "What is the price at 500?" The product is listed. The buyer needs the answer, now.

Configuring and quoting sounds different. "Will this actuator fit my compressor?" The buyer picks options. Some combinations are wrong. A wrong one means a return, or worse.

Most distributors with listed SKUs are in the first group. Most makers of compatibility-bound equipment are in the second. Many are in both.

Run this three-question self-test:

- Can a buyer purchase the item exactly as listed, or must options be chosen first?
- Does a wrong combination cause a return, a rework or a safety issue?
- Does your catalog live in files, or in a PIM or ERP with a data team behind it?

Two "options" or "wrong combination" answers point to configuring and quoting. Mostly "as listed" and "files" points to finding and asking.

## What does each one do with a PDF or Excel catalog?

Both can start from files. ChatSKU is built around them. Zoovu reads PDFs too, but as one input to a broader data platform.

ChatSKU accepts CSV files with column mapping, PDF catalogs where AI extracts the products, manual entry and HTML listings. You can be answering questions the same day. The [ChatSKU features](https://chatsku.com/features/) page lists what connects.

The free trial has limits on file size and catalog size, so check them before you test.

Zoovu's data platform collects product data from PDFs, web pages, reviews, PIMs and ERPs. It then cleans and standardizes that data. [Zoovu's B2B Site Search page](https://zoovu.com/solution/b2b-site-search) describes one source of truth built from those inputs (checked 2026-11-05).

So the file format is not the real difference. The scope of the data work around it is.

Zoovu builds a cleaned, standardized data layer. ChatSKU starts from the catalog you can hand over today. If you have no PIM and no data team, that distinction matters. ## How do quotes differ, a request or a configuration?

ChatSKU takes a quote request from a buyer and hands it to your team. Zoovu lets a buyer configure the product and then request the quote. For configurable lines, Zoovu is the stronger fit.

With ChatSKU, a buyer submits a request in chat with a name, email and company. Your team reviews it in the dashboard. They apply pricing and discounts, add notes and send it back.

Discounts can be set by customer group on paid plans. Quotes export to CSV.

With Zoovu, the buyer does more of the work up front. Its self-service RFQ tool lets buyers find specs, configure products, bundles or BOMs, and request a quote. Its Visual CPQ page lists compatibility mapping, automatic quote generation and PDF download of a configuration.

Its industrial CPQ page describes ERP pricing and approvals for complex products (checked 2026-11-05).

Say a buyer needs a price on a listed part after hours. A quote request in chat lands in your queue with the product and quantity attached. Nobody has to configure anything.

Now picture a buyer who must build a valid assembly. A request alone does not help. The configuration is the product.

## When does Zoovu fit better, and when does ChatSKU?

Zoovu fits better when products are configurable and you want a full discovery and data layer. ChatSKU fits better when your catalog is in files and the job is answering buyers and capturing quote requests.

Zoovu is the stronger fit in these cases. Your products are compatibility-bound, so buyers must build a valid configuration. You run an enterprise stack such as SAP and want a discovery layer across it.

You also need dealer syndication, or SOC 2 Type 2 is a procurement requirement. That is Zoovu's own claim. ChatSKU's pages state encryption, GDPR and data processing agreements, not SOC 2. And you can fund a project that runs several weeks.

ChatSKU is the stronger fit in other cases. Your catalog sits in PDF, Excel or CSV files, or on a plain site. The job is answering buyers and capturing quote requests, including after hours.

You want to be live in a day and try it free. You want published plans. And you have no PIM or data team.

Where do they overlap? A distributor with simple SKUs and customer-specific prices could use either for answers. The difference is how much structure and project effort you want to take on.

You may want both. A manufacturer with configurable lines can use a configurator for those lines. The same company may still want an after-hours catalog assistant for everything else.

## What is the best AI product discovery for B2B distributors?

No single tool wins for every distributor. It depends on your catalog shape. Rule of thumb: configure and quote, choose depth. Find and ask, choose speed.

A distributor that sells varied options from several manufacturers, with structured data behind it, gets more from a discovery and CPQ layer. Zoovu's Visual CPQ page names distributors and resellers among its audiences (checked 2026-11-05).

A distributor that sells listed SKUs from PDFs or spreadsheets gets more from a catalog assistant. The buyer asks, gets an answer and requests a quote.

Use the three-question test from earlier. It takes five minutes and saves a long evaluation.

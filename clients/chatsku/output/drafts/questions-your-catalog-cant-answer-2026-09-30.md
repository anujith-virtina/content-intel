<h2>Executive summary</h2>
<p>Your catalog answers the questions you built it to answer. Buyers ask different ones, in their own words. A search box only matches strings, not intent, so it misses most of what a real buyer types.</p>
<p>This post covers six question shapes that show up in almost every B2B catalog. A part number typed with the wrong punctuation. A competitor's cross-reference. An application instead of a product name. A compound spec string. A pack size. An old number nobody updated.</p>
<p>Each one breaks a search box for a slightly different reason. Each one needs something different to actually get answered. We'll walk through what that takes, what it costs you when nobody does it, and what to do about it this week.</p>

<h2>Introduction</h2>
<p>A buyer lands on your site at 9pm. She needs a stainless fitting, three-quarter inch, NPT thread. She types "316 stainless 3/4 npt" into your search box.</p>
<p>Nothing comes back. She tries "3/4 npt stainless" instead. Still nothing.</p>
<p>She gives up and emails your competitor. He answers first thing in the morning. By then, she's already ordered from someone else.</p>
<p>Nobody on your team hears about any of this. No ticket gets filed. No rep gets a callback. The only record is a line in a report nobody reads.</p>
<p>That buyer asked a real question, in plain words. Your catalog just couldn't answer it. This happens more often than you think, and in six specific shapes.</p>

<p>Six shapes, one thread running through them. Every one needs something past matching text. Reading what a buyer means, connecting it to what you actually sell, and finding the right SKU no matter how it got typed.</p>

<h2>Why can't my search box handle these?</h2>
<p>Because a search box matches strings. It compares the letters a buyer typed against the letters in your product data, and either they line up or they don't.</p>
<p>There's no in-between. A search box has no idea "pump for pool" and "submersible pool pump" mean the same thing. Someone has to tell it, one pair at a time.</p>
<p>That's not a bug you patch with a bigger index. It's the whole design. <a href="/traditional-b2b-product-search-fails/">Why keyword search fails</a> covers the mechanics behind this in full, if you want the deeper version.</p>
<p>What matters here is what changes once something can actually read the question, instead of just matching it.</p>

<h2>How would I even know this is happening?</h2>
<p>You probably already have a record of it. Your search box keeps a log of everything typed into it, answered or not.</p>
<p>GA4 keeps its own search term report. Magento ships a built-in Search Terms report. WooCommerce doesn't track this natively at all, so you'd need a plugin or GA4 to see it there.</p>
<p>You don't need to become a data analyst to use this. You just need to know it exists, and that the six patterns above are probably already sitting in it.</p>

<h2>People also ask</h2>
<h3>Do buyers really search using part numbers and cross-references?</h3>
<p>Yes, constantly. B2B buyers often know a competitor's or an OEM's part number better than yours. If that string isn't matched to one of your SKUs, your search box has nothing to work with.</p>
<h3>Why does application language cause zero results?</h3>
<p>Because a buyer describes a job, not a product name. Your catalog copy says "submersible pump." A buyer searching for one types "pump for pool." Those are two different strings to a search box.</p>
<h3>Can a bigger product catalog fix this on its own?</h3>
<p>No. More SKUs means more of these questions, not fewer. The fix is in how a question gets read and answered, not how many products sit behind it.</p>

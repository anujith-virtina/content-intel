Summary

Quick answer: use a form when the buyer knows the part number and quantity. Use a chat when the buyer has a spec but no part number, or asks after hours. Use both when both feed one quote queue.

Then measure the same four timestamps on each channel. No independent head-to-head test of form versus chat exists. So this guide ends with a pilot you can run on your own traffic.

Introduction

Two buyers open your store the same mid-morning. One has a part number and a quantity. She wants a price.

The other has a written spec, no part number and no idea which item fits. Both land on the same quote form.

It suits the first buyer well. It stalls the second.

Owners ask the same question at this point. Should the store use an RFQ form, a chat or both?

This guide gives you a rule for choosing, a way to connect the two and a way to measure them. It also says plainly where the evidence runs out.

Why do RFQ forms lose buyers?

RFQ forms lose buyers when they ask for details the buyer lacks, or for more than the price needs. The loss is quiet. The buyer closes the tab, and you never see the request.

What happens when the buyer has a spec but no part number?

Go back to the two buyers. Buyer A has a part number and a quantity. Buyer B has a written spec for a stainless fastener and no part number.

A required part number field is fine for buyer A. For buyer B it is a locked door. She is the buyer who most needs help, and the form gives her the least.

Searching first often fails too. Better ecommerce site search helps buyer B find a candidate item before any quote step. Then the form is not her only way in.

Why do long forms stall?

Nielsen Norman Group advises cutting any field whose data can be derived, collected later or dropped. It cut one of its own forms from six fields to two.

Baymard reported in June 2024 that the average checkout has 11.3 fields. Most checkouts need about eight.

Both sources cover general web forms and consumer checkout, which is B2C evidence. Neither ties field count to RFQ conversion. Treat them as direction, not proof.

What makes a good RFQ form?

A good RFQ form asks only for what changes the price and fills in everything it already knows. That rule sounds easy. It is hard to hold, because every team wants one more field.

What should the form ask for?

Ask for what changes the price, and nothing else. Make every other field optional and label it as optional. Nielsen Norman Group suggests keeping optional fields to one or two.

Setup steps, product routing and the full field list are covered in the WooCommerce quote request workflow write-up. This guide stays on the choice of channel.

Which fields can the store fill in for you?

Logged-in buyers should never retype what the store already knows. A B2B customer portal can pre-fill company and contact details on every request.

The form can also show customer-specific price and stock, read from your ERP. Then the buyer sees a real answer before she submits anything.

A form has one honest limit. It works when the buyer already knows what to ask for.

How short is short enough?

No source gives a fixed field count for RFQ forms. A CHI 2014 study, reported by Nielsen Norman Group, tested general web forms.

On forms that followed good guidelines, 78% of users submitted without errors on the first try. On forms that did not, 42% did.

That is not RFQ data. It does show that clear, short forms are easier to finish.

Keep the form for known part numbers and repeat buyers.

When is chat better than a form?

Chat is better than a form when the buyer cannot name the part yet, or asks after your team's hours. A chat asks one question at a time. A form shows every question at once.

Which requests suit a chat?

Buyer B is the clear case. She has a spec and no part number. A chat can ask a follow-up question, point to matching items and then gather quantity and date.

Chat also covers questions that arrive after hours, when nobody on your team is at a desk.

A chat has limits. Firm custom pricing and unusual jobs belong with a person. Lead-time and stock answers also depend on the store holding that data, as the notes on backorder lead times show.

What does a catalog chat add to the quote request?

A form leaves one gap. It cannot help the buyer who does not know what to ask for.

Picture buyer B typing her spec. A chat can ask for quantity and timeline, then pass a complete request to the quote queue.

A chat works best on clean, structured product data, so catalog, pricing and permissions need to be set up first.

Firm pricing and complex custom jobs still go to a person. The chat hands off to the rep and does not replace the rep.

Use chat for spec-heavy or unclear needs and after-hours questions.

How do the store, the form and the chat work together?

The form and the chat work together when both read the same product data and write to one quote queue. Two channels with two back ends create two problems. Reps price from two lists, and buyers get different answers.

What do both channels share?

Four parts sit behind both channels:

  • Product data. clear titles, specs, units and pack sizes.
  • Price and stock. read from your ERP through a WooCommerce ERP integration.
  • Routing rules. who gets which request.
  • One quote queue. the single list your reps work from.

Keeping these four parts in order is the store owner's job. It is also the part Virtina builds. Magento teams can read how Adobe Commerce negotiable quotes handle multi-round price talks.

Where should a chat-started quote land?

It should land in the same queue as form requests. The fields should be complete, and the conversation should be attached. Then the rep prices one list, not two inboxes.

A chat that writes to a separate inbox doubles the work.

What stays with a person?

Firm pricing on unusual jobs, approvals and negotiated terms stay with a person. The chat should hand off cleanly, with the context attached. The rep should never ask the buyer to start over.

Which buyer goes where?

Buyer A, with a part number and a quantity, goes to the form. Buyer B, with a spec and no part number, goes to the chat. A repeat buyer inside an account sees the form pre-filled.

The form wins on simplicity and control. The chat wins when the need is unclear.

How do you test it on your own traffic?

No independent head-to-head test of form versus chat exists. The evidence available comes from vendors, and each vendor sells one side. A pilot on your own requests is the only fair answer.

Run both channels into one queue. Compare the same four measures on each. Pick the duration with your sales team.

How do you measure quote response time?

You measure quote response time by stamping the same four moments on every request, whichever channel it came from. Do it for forms and chats alike. Then the two compare fairly.

Which four timestamps?

Use request received, first acknowledgment, priced quote sent and quote accepted. This is a plain way to define it, not an industry standard.

From those four moments you can track four measures. They are the median time to first reply and the median time to a priced quote. The other two are the after-hours share and the share needing a follow-up for missing data.

Set no target until you see your own numbers.

Calendar clock or business clock?

Zendesk, a helpdesk tool, keeps calendar-hour and business-hour values for each ticket. Business-hour reporting needs a configured schedule and is not retroactive. The idea carries over to quotes.

An evening request looks fast on a business clock, because the clock has not started. It looks slow on a calendar clock. Track both.

How do you tell a started quote from a finished one?

Google Analytics 4 enhanced measurement fires form_start once per session, on first interaction with a form. It fires form_submit when the form is submitted. Started minus submitted gives you an abandonment signal.

A chat has no HTML form, so it likely needs a custom "quote started" event. That is an inference, not a documented rule.

Site search works the same way. GA4 fires view_search_results when a results page loads. It does not appear to log zero results on its own, which is also an inference.

Is a chatbot better than an RFQ form?

Neither is better in every case. A form fits known part numbers and repeat buyers. A chat fits unclear needs and after-hours questions.

How many fields should an RFQ form have?

No fixed number is backed by a source. Cut every field that does not change the price. Keep optional fields to one or two.

Can a chat give a firm price?

Only if it reads price from your ERP and your pricing rules. Otherwise it should hand the request to a person. Unusual jobs always belong with your sales team.

Frequently asked questions

What is an RFQ form?

An RFQ form is a web form where a business buyer asks for a price on specific products. A person then prices the request and replies. It is the standard first step for items without a public price.

Should a small B2B store start with a form or a chat?

Start with a good form if most requests include a part number. Add a chat when buyers often arrive with a spec or ask after hours. Your own request history will show which case is more common.

Do I need to replace my current form to add a chat?

No, keep the form for known part numbers and repeat buyers, and add the chat beside it. Route both into one quote queue.

What data does a chat need to quote well?

It needs clean product data, price rules, stock and buyer permissions. Without them, the chat guesses. A quick answer that is wrong costs more than a slower one that is right.

Can a chat replace my sales rep?

No. A chat handles routine questions and gathers a complete request. It hands off to your rep for firm pricing, approvals and unusual jobs.

How do I know my quote response is slow?

Stamp four moments on every request: received, first acknowledgment, priced quote sent and accepted. Read them on a calendar clock and a business clock. Compare your own numbers over time.

How long should a pilot run?

No number is sourced, so set it with your sales team. Run it long enough to see both repeat buyers and new buyers with specs. Compare the same four measures on the form and the chat.

Summary

Your Magento version fell out of support on 11 August 2026. The store still runs and the licence stays valid. Adobe also states that an unsupported version is no longer PCI compliant.

Four responses are available: upgrade to a supported 2.4.x, move to Adobe Commerce Cloud, replatform, or document the risk. For most merchants the right move is the upgrade, not a replatform. This guide walks all four, starting with the one that earns us the least.

Introduction

Two things happened to Magento on 11 August 2026, and most merchants only noticed one of them. The 2.4.6 line left regular support that day, per Adobe's published release documentation. On the same date, 2.4.5 left extended support, its final phase of ordinary coverage.

A month later nothing has broken, which is exactly what makes this decision hard. Your board wants a position, your agency has an interest in the answer, and your budget cycle is open. The deadline is real, but the panic version of it is not.

This article walks four paths, ordered by how little each one earns us. Every date below comes from Adobe's own documentation, checked on 10 September 2026.

What changed for Magento on 11 August 2026?

On 11 August 2026 Adobe stopped issuing quality fixes and security patches for the 2.4.6 line. Adobe's published release documentation puts 2.4.5 out of extended support on the same date. That was 2.4.5's last phase of ordinary coverage.

Which Magento versions are still supported?

The 2.4.7, 2.4.8 and 2.4.9 release lines all remain in regular support today. The next cliff is 31 May 2027, when 2.4.7 exits regular support. If you are on 2.4.7, that is the date to budget against now.

What Adobe stops doing after the end-of-support date

Your store keeps running and your licence stays valid, per Adobe's end-of-support FAQ. Adobe calls this end of support (EOS), not end of life (EOL), and the difference is real.

Tickets raised after the date are not resolved, and no further product changes are made. That includes security and PCI compliance changes, and community pull requests are no longer merged.

Adobe's lifecycle policy sets a three-year standard support window from each version's GA date. The same policy states that Adobe does not patch third-party dependencies such as PHP and MySQL.

Are you out of PCI compliance now?

Adobe says yes, and that is Adobe's published position on its own software. Your acquiring bank, not Adobe, is the party that acts on it.

What Adobe states about unsupported versions

Adobe's end-of-support FAQ, checked on 10 September 2026, states that an unsupported version is "no longer PCI compliant." The same FAQ names fines, loss of card processing ability, or other penalties as consequences. That is the platform owner saying it about the platform it owns.

We are reporting Adobe's statement, not a clause of the PCI DSS standard itself. We did not read that standard, so we will not name a requirement number.

Why your acquiring bank and QSA decide this

The practical consequence does not run through Adobe at all. It runs through your acquiring bank and your qualified security assessor, who judge your PCI DSS compliance. They decide whether an unsupported release line is a finding, and on what timetable.

So confirm your own position with your QSA before you assume either safety or catastrophe. A QSA conversation costs an hour. Assuming is the expensive option.

What we could and could not verify about edition differences

Adobe's published versions page lists the lifecycle dates without drawing a distinction between editions. We checked that page on 10 September 2026 and it does not mention Magento Open Source at all. Several agency articles assert that extended-support patches reach Adobe Commerce customers only.

We could not verify that split against Adobe's own documentation, so we will not repeat it as fact. Confirm your own edition's dates on Adobe's published versions page before you plan around extended support.

Then apply the safe planning rule. If you cannot confirm that you receive extended support, plan as though 11 August 2026 was your end date.

What actually drives upgrade cost and effort

An upgrade quote is built from six lines, and none of them is your revenue. Those six are extension re-certification, module remediation, theme rework, integration re-testing, infrastructure changes, and QA with UAT. Five numbers price all six.

  • Installed extensions. Each one needs re-certification on the target version.
  • Core overrides. Remediation scales with how far you forked core.
  • Store views. Each view multiplies theme, content and QA work.
  • Live integrations. ERP, PIM, tax and gateway all need re-testing.
  • PHP distance. A big jump is where frontend rework bites.

QA and UAT is the line most quotes under-scope. Budget a staging environment and a rollback plan into the same figure. Our Magento development services team scopes upgrades against those five numbers.

Path 3: replatform to a different eCommerce platform

Leaving is right when the upgrade has stopped being a version bump and become a rebuild. Every upgrade is a project rather than maintenance, and total cost of ownership is dominated by keeping the platform alive.

Or you cannot staff it at a price you will pay. Or your requirements genuinely simplified since you chose Magento, which happens more often than vendors admit. If three of those describe you, put eCommerce replatforming services on the table properly.

Five store profiles that should stay on Magento

Five profiles should stay, for structural reasons.

  • Complex B2B catalogues. Company accounts, contract pricing and approval hierarchies are native, as Adobe Commerce B2B features shows.
  • Multi-store hierarchies. Website, store and store-view structures have no clean equivalent, as Magento 2 multistore configuration shows.
  • Deep ERP integration. A working, tested connector is the asset, so Magento ERP integration work would restart elsewhere.
  • Large attributed catalogues. Many attribute combinations do not map cleanly to simpler models.
  • Mid-peak timing. Nobody should cut a store over between October and January.

If two or more describe you, upgrade.

Where merchants who do leave usually land

Most merchants who leave Magento land on WooCommerce, and we will name that rather than list five options neutrally. It suits merchants whose requirements simplified and who want ownership of the stack. Our Magento vs WooCommerce comparison sets out where each one wins.

Path 4: document the risk and buy a quarter

If you cannot move before your next peak, the honest position is not that you are fine. It is that you write the risk down. Most merchants are on this path right now.

What a defensible risk acceptance contains

Four things make it defensible instead of decorative. Tell your acquiring bank and your QSA exactly where you are, in writing. Apply the hardening you can at the WAF and infrastructure layer, which our Magento store security guide covers.

Put a dated commitment in front of your board, with a target release and a quarter.

2.4.6 stores sit better here than 2.4.5 stores. 2.4.6 still carries extended support to 31 August 2027 and security provisioning to 31 May 2028. Adobe calls that extension a one-time exception, so do not plan a second one.

2.4.5's last ordinary phase ended on 11 August 2026. A documented, dated, temporary position is defensible. Silence is not.

What should you check this week?

Five checks, all of which you can finish before Friday, decide which path you are on.

  • Version and edition. Confirm both exactly, from the admin, not from memory.
  • Third-party extensions. Count them and list the vendors behind them.
  • Store views and websites. Count both, because each multiplies the work.
  • Live integrations. List every one, including ERP, PIM, tax and gateway.
  • Current PHP version. Compare it against your target version's requirement.

Then check the second clock most readers have forgotten. php.net ends security support for PHP 8.2 on 31 December 2026. An unsupported Magento version running PHP 8.2 has two clocks expiring within five months of each other.

People also ask

Does Magento stop working after end of support?

No. The software keeps running and the licence stays valid while your account is in good standing. Adobe's end-of-support FAQ says so directly.

What stops is patching, ticket resolution and further product changes. Nothing changes on your storefront on the date itself.

Is Magento 2.4.6 still safe to run in 2026?

Not indefinitely. Every vulnerability found after 11 August 2026 is patched on supported lines and left open on yours. The public fix effectively documents the flaw.

Supported lines get the patch and yours does not. Risk compounds from here rather than decaying.

How long does a Magento version upgrade take?

Agencies typically quote 2 to 6 weeks for a straightforward version upgrade. That is what agencies quote, not a figure we can verify. Effort scales with extension count, core overrides and store views.

Your own numbers will move that range in either direction.

Conclusion

Most readers should upgrade to a supported 2.4.x and stay on Magento. That is the smallest project on the list and the one we profit from least. The deadline is real and the panic version of it is not.

Replatforming is right for a narrow profile you can name in a sentence. If complex B2B, multi-store or a working ERP connector describes you, upgrade instead. And if you cannot move this quarter, write the risk down with a date on it.

Frequently Asked Questions

When did Magento 2.4.6 lose support?

11 August 2026, per Adobe's published release documentation. That date is regular support, the phase that covers quality fixes and security patches. Ignore the March 2026 date that circulates in agency coverage, because Adobe's own page contradicts it.

Do I have to replatform because there is no upgrade path?

No, and Adobe documents direct upgrades to supported 2.4.x release lines. The no-upgrade-path framing comes from parties who benefit when replatforming looks inevitable. Check who wrote the article before you accept the premise.

That test applies to this article too.

Should I upgrade to Magento 2.4.8 or 2.4.9?

2.4.8 if you carry a heavy extension footprint, because PHP 8.4 and 8.3 have broader vendor support. 2.4.9 if you want the extra year and your extensions already certify on PHP 8.5. Adobe's system requirements are the place to confirm both before you commit.

Does an unsupported version break PCI compliance?

Adobe's end-of-support FAQ states that an unsupported version is no longer PCI compliant. We are reporting Adobe's position on its own software, not a named clause of PCI DSS. Confirm your own position with your QSA and your acquiring bank before acting.

Does Magento Open Source get the same extended support as Adobe Commerce?

Adobe's versions page lists the lifecycle dates without drawing an edition split. Some agency coverage claims one, and we could not verify it.

Confirm your edition's dates on Adobe's versions page. If you cannot confirm extended support, plan as though 11 August 2026 was your end date.

What PHP version does Magento 2.4.9 need?

PHP 8.5, per Adobe's system requirements documentation. 2.4.8 accepts PHP 8.4 or 8.3, which is why it is often the safer target. Check your extension vendors' compatibility matrices before you pick either one.

When does Magento 2.4.7 lose regular support?

31 May 2027, per Adobe's published release documentation. Extended support for 2.4.7 runs to 31 May 2028. If you are on 2.4.7 today, that is the deadline you should be budgeting against now.

How do you rank B2B marketplaces, and what did we leave out?

We ranked nine marketplaces by one number: Ahrefs' estimate of monthly US organic search visits, read on 2 November 2026. That number orders this list. Every figure carries the label Ahrefs estimate, US organic visits, 2 November 2026.

Each entry then gets a verdict on fit. The verdicts are our judgment, not a measured score.

What the traffic number can and cannot tell you

The figures are modeled estimates, not exact counts. They cover organic search only, so they undercount direct, app, email, and logged-in buying.

Amazon Business is measured on its business.amazon.com subdomain only. Visits that begin on amazon.com are not counted, so its rank is understated.

No figure separates business buyers from other visitors. Search visits are not revenue, orders, or supplier quality.

US search also favors US-facing sites. IndiaMART and Ankorstore serve India and Europe, so their low US numbers say little about their size at home.

Which sites are left out, and why

We left out consumer marketplaces, build-your-own marketplace software, and retail-first sites. For the consumer side, see the ranking of top online shopping sites. We counted one domain per company.

The nine are a candidate set drawn from names that recur in competing lists. It is not a census of every marketplace.

Tundra and Wholesale Central recur in other lists, but we did not pull their traffic. They are unranked, and that says nothing about where they would land.

1. Alibaba.com, 953K visits: biggest search draw, not the purest B2B

Alibaba.com is an open marketplace with a request for quotation (RFQ) tool. A buyer posts a request and receives quotes from several suppliers. Alibaba calls itself the world's largest online B2B marketplace, which is a company claim.

Its Trade Assurance program, per the company, backs orders with secure payment, guaranteed delivery, and a money-back guarantee. Minimum order quantity (MOQ) is set per listing, with examples of 2 and 50 on the homepage. The page states no platform-wide minimum.

Verdict: strongest for a buyer sourcing finished goods across borders. For a US seller it is an export channel, and buyers must check each supplier's MOQ. The top traffic rank reflects broad search appeal, not B2B purity.

2. DHgate, 702K visits: popular, but read the terms yourself

DHgate gets an estimated 702K US organic visits a month (Ahrefs estimate, 2 November 2026). We could not verify DHgate's terms from its own pages, so we state nothing about minimums, protections, or fees.

Second by search traffic does not make it second best for a business buyer. Read its terms yourself before you place an order. Treat the rank as a measure of search appeal only.

3. Faire, 275K visits: the closest fit for independent retailers

Faire's own About page says the company was created in 2017. It also states a mission to modernize wholesale between brands and independent retailers.

That intent is the closest match for an independent retailer, or a brand selling to retailers. Confirm every term on Faire's seller and retailer pages before you rely on it. We make no claims here about its terms or scale.

4. Made-in-China.com, 165K visits: a China sourcing route with homework

Made-in-China.com is a sourcing directory with online ordering. You find products and audited suppliers, connect, then order online. One RFQ can bring several quotes.

The company reports 2.5 million+ suppliers and 110 million+ products. A secured trading service holds payment until delivery, in the company's wording. It also runs an audited supplier program, so open each supplier's audit report.

Verdict: useful for sourcing from China if you budget the inspection work. Landed cost, customs, and quality control sit with the buyer.

5. Amazon Business, 121K visits (understated): built for buyers with controls

Amazon Business is an open marketplace layered on Amazon retail, and its rank is understated. Ahrefs measured business.amazon.com only. Visits that begin on amazon.com are not counted.

Buyer tools include approval workflows, buying groups, user permissions, and spend reports. Buyers also see quantity discounts, case packs, and several payment methods. Sellers can offer business-only prices, list case packs and pallets, and answer quote requests.

On the Amazon Business seller program page, a Professional account costs $39.99 a month plus selling fees. We read that page on 2 November 2026.

Verdict: the best fit for organizations buying everyday supplies with controls. For sellers, the customer relationship stays with Amazon.

6. IndiaMART, 93.8K visits: a sourcing channel for India

IndiaMART is an India-focused open marketplace and directory. It calls itself India's largest online B2B marketplace, which is a company claim. Its page says sellers list for free by adding a business and at least three products or services.

The page also mentions verified sellers, payment protection, and a lead feed called BuyLead. A US buyer reaches suppliers cross-border, and the page does not describe US shipping or customs help. Low US traffic says little about its size at home.

Verdict: a sourcing channel for India, not a US channel.

7. ThomasNet, 85.4K visits: an inquiry engine, not a checkout

ThomasNet is an industrial sourcing directory for North America, and it has no checkout. Buyers search, shortlist, send requests for information, and get quotes from several suppliers at once. The ThomasNet About page reports over 125 years in operation and more than 500,000 supplier profiles.

Suppliers can pay for visibility through a monthly advertising program, where they pick keywords and a budget.

Verdict: it starts an inquiry for the buyer. For the supplier, the order happens elsewhere, so your quote-to-order flow is the bottleneck.

8. Global Sources, 28.2K visits: an RFQ and trade show route

Global Sources shows three things on its homepage: request for quotation, trade shows, and supplier listings with years in business. Its About page returned an error, so we claim nothing else.

Verdict: an RFQ-and-trade-show sourcing route. Check each listing yourself before you commit.

9. Ankorstore, 3.9K visits: low US traffic, a European story

Ankorstore's company blog, dated 21 May 2024, says it serves independent retailers and brands in Europe. It reports 300,000+ retailers across Europe and 30,000+ European brands. The same post cites a EUR 100 minimum order and payment up to 60 days for eligible retailers.

We could not confirm current terms or US availability. Low US traffic reflects geography. Verdict: relevant if you sell to European independents, otherwise skip it.

A marketplace where buyers order in an app, through EDI, or in a logged-in account is undercounted here. Search data only sees visits that start on a public page.

Amazon Business is the clearest case, because Ahrefs measured only its business.amazon.com subdomain. Direct visits, app orders, and email reorders never show in organic search. Regional sites such as IndiaMART and Ankorstore also look small through a US filter.

Marketplaces we did not rank

Tundra and Wholesale Central recur in other lists, but we did not pull their traffic. They are unranked, and no position is implied. Other regional players are absent for the same reason.

Which type of B2B marketplace fits your business?

Pick by model first: directories send inquiries, open marketplaces take orders, and wholesale marketplaces match brands with retailers. Traffic comes second. The model decides what happens after a buyer finds you.

If you sell to independent retailers

Faire and Ankorstore are the two wholesale marketplaces in this ranking, as their own pages describe them. Faire states a mission to modernize wholesale between brands and independent retailers. Ankorstore's 2024 blog says it serves independent retailers and brands in Europe.

If you make or distribute industrial goods

ThomasNet, Made-in-China.com, and Global Sources send an inquiry or an RFQ. The order happens somewhere else. That is where B2B ecommerce for manufacturers matters, because the quote has to become an order you can fulfill.

If you buy for a business

Amazon Business lists approval workflows and spend reports for organizations buying supplies. Alibaba offers an RFQ tool for sourcing finished goods. On either, verify each supplier before the first purchase order.

Should you sell on a marketplace or run your own B2B store?

Do both: marketplaces find the first order, and your own store holds pricing, net terms, reorders, and ERP sync. A marketplace is acquisition. Your store is retention.

Who owns the buyer after the first order?

Ask who holds the buyer's contact details and reorder history once the first order ships. On your own store, that data is yours. A separate piece on marketplace-only sales risk covers that dependency argument in full.

Repeat buyers want self-serve reorders, invoices, and order history. A B2B customer portal gives them that without a call to your sales desk.

Where do negotiated prices and net terms live?

Negotiated prices live in account-specific pricing on your own store. B2B pricing rules tie price lists, tiers, and minimums to customer groups. Each marketplace sets its own pricing and payment terms, so read its seller pages.

Net terms, purchase orders, and card or invoice payment depend on the gateways you connect. Compare payment gateway providers before you promise terms to a buyer.

How does a marketplace order reach your ERP?

A directory inquiry must become a quote, then an order, then an ERP record. That quote-to-order path is the step to design first. Scope it with ecommerce integration services that map items, prices, and customers to the ERP.

Teams ready to build can start with B2B ecommerce development. If the catalog already runs on WooCommerce, WooCommerce development services are the shorter path.

Five questions to put to any marketplace

Put these five questions to any marketplace before you list. They apply to every platform in this ranking.

  • Buyer data. Who owns the buyer's contact details after the first order?
  • Negotiated prices. Does the platform support account-specific pricing?
  • Net terms. Are net terms and purchase orders supported?
  • ERP sync. Does order data sync to your ERP?
  • Suspension. What happens to your revenue if the account is suspended?

People also ask

What is a B2B marketplace?

A B2B marketplace is an online platform where businesses buy from and sell to other businesses. Models differ. Open marketplaces take orders, sourcing directories pass on inquiries, and wholesale marketplaces match brands with retailers.

Which B2B marketplace gets the most US search traffic?

Alibaba.com, at an estimated 953K US organic visits a month (Ahrefs estimate, 2 November 2026). DHgate follows at 702K and Faire at 275K. These are search visits, not sales.

Is ThomasNet a marketplace?

ThomasNet is a sourcing directory, not a marketplace with a checkout. Buyers search, shortlist, and request quotes from suppliers. The order happens outside the platform.

Can Amazon Business buyers use approval workflows?

Yes. Amazon lists centralized approval workflows among its buyer tools, alongside buying groups and user permissions. Check your own account for current options.

Conclusion

Search traffic shows where buyers look first. The model decides fit, and the repeat order belongs on a store you own.

Pick the marketplace whose model matches how you sell. Then write down the five questions and put them to it before you list.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a B2B marketplace and a sourcing directory?

A marketplace takes the order on its own platform. A sourcing directory connects you to suppliers, and the order happens elsewhere. ThomasNet is a directory with no checkout.

Does Amazon Business offer net terms?

The Amazon pages we read do not state it. Check with Amazon before you assume it either way. We make no claim here.

Is Alibaba a B2B or B2C marketplace?

Alibaba describes itself as a B2B marketplace. Traffic data cannot separate business buyers from other visitors. Treat its rank as search appeal, not proof of business-only use.

Does a higher traffic rank mean a better marketplace?

No. Traffic measures search visibility, not revenue, orders, or supplier quality. A smaller platform can fit your model better.

Can you trust these traffic figures?

Treat them as modeled estimates. They come from Ahrefs Site Explorer with a US filter, read on 2 November 2026. They cover organic search only, so they undercount direct, app, and logged-in buying.

Should a manufacturer sell on a marketplace or its own store?

Use both, for different jobs. Marketplaces and directories help buyers find you. Your own store holds pricing, net terms, reorders, and ERP sync.

What should you check before listing on any B2B marketplace?

Ask who owns the buyer data, whether negotiated prices and net terms work, and how orders reach your ERP. Read the platform's own seller terms. Confirm what happens to your revenue if the account is suspended.

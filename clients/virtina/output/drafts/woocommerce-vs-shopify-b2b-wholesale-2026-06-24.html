What is B2B wholesale ecommerce?

B2B wholesale ecommerce is the online sale of products from manufacturers, distributors, and wholesalers to other businesses. Those buyers are retailers, resellers, or institutional buyers. It's structurally different from consumer retail at every level.

A B2C buyer picks a product, pays, and ships. A B2B wholesale buyer negotiates pricing, submits purchase orders, expects net payment terms, and may manage multiple delivery locations under one account.

RFQ (Request for Quote)

An RFQ is a formal buyer request for pricing on a specific product and quantity before placing an order. It's essential for distributors with negotiated pricing, irregular volumes, or custom order requirements.

On WooCommerce, B2BKing handles RFQ natively. The buyer submits a request, the sales rep negotiates within the platform, and the approved quote converts directly to an order with PDF export. On Shopify Plus, RFQ requires a third-party app.

MOQ (Minimum Order Quantity)

MOQ is the minimum number of units or order value a buyer must purchase per transaction. It's enforced at the product level, category level, or cart level depending on the platform and plugin.

WooCommerce enforces MOQ at all three levels through B2BKing with role-based rules. Shopify Plus offers quantity rules natively on the Plus plan; standard plans require an app.

Net terms

Net terms are deferred payment arrangements: the buyer pays within a set number of days after the invoice date. Net 30 means payment is due 30 days after invoicing.

Shopify Plus now offers native Net 15, Net 30, Net 60, and Net 90 terms, assignable per company with overdue tracking built in. On WooCommerce, B2BKing handles custom payment terms and invoice generation without requiring upfront payment at checkout.

Tax exemption

Tax exemption allows distributors to sell to resellers or government entities without charging sales tax, when a valid exemption certificate is on file. B2BKing handles VAT and tax exemption by role, with certificate collection integrated into the registration workflow.

On Shopify Plus, the "Don't collect tax" flag is available at the company location level natively. Certificate collection, validation, and audit-trail management require Avalara, ExemptSync, or an equivalent service.

Tiered and customer-specific pricing

Tiered pricing varies by customer role, order volume, or individual account. B2BKing supports role-based, product-level, category-level, and user-specific pricing overrides. Wholesale Suite, another leading WooCommerce plugin, handles quantity-based pricing rules and role-based discounts.

Shopify Plus uses price lists assigned per company or location. Unlimited price lists are available on the Plus plan; standard plans are capped at three catalogs.

ERP integration

ERP integration connects the ecommerce platform to an Enterprise Resource Planning system to sync inventory, customer accounts, orders, and pricing in real time. Neither WooCommerce nor Shopify has a universal native ERP connector.

Both platforms require middleware or custom API work for systems like SAP Business One, Oracle NetSuite, Epicor P21, or Microsoft Dynamics. Virtina scopes these integrations as infrastructure-level custom development, not plugin swaps.

How does each platform handle B2B wholesale workflows?

The four workflows below are where WooCommerce and Shopify Plus diverge most significantly for wholesale distributors. Understanding the differences at this level is more useful than comparing feature lists.

Step 1: Customer registration and approval

On WooCommerce with B2BKing, you build custom registration forms with required fields specific to your wholesale onboarding process. Accounts sit in a pending state until manually approved. Once approved, the account is assigned a wholesale role, and pricing, catalog visibility, and checkout rules activate automatically.

On Shopify Plus, wholesale buyers are added through company profiles. An admin manually creates the company, assigns contacts, and controls location-level permissions.

There is no native automated registration and approval flow on Shopify. If you need a self-serve application process, you'll need an app or a custom Shopify Flow setup.

Step 2: Pricing and catalog setup

WooCommerce with B2BKing offers role-based pricing, user-specific price overrides, and tiered or volume-based pricing at the product, category, and cart level. Catalog visibility rules let you hide prices, hide products, or remove the add-to-cart button for guest or retail visitors by role.

Shopify Plus uses price lists assigned per company or location. On Plus, you get unlimited price lists. On standard plans, you're limited to three.

The pricing system is less granular than B2BKing at the individual user level, but it's native and doesn't require plugin management.

Step 3: Order workflow including RFQ

WooCommerce with B2BKing includes a dedicated wholesale order form. It's a one-page catalog view where buyers add multiple products without navigating individual product pages.

The native RFQ system lets buyers submit a quote request, and the sales rep negotiates pricing within the platform. The approved quote converts to an order with PDF export. No third-party app is required.

Shopify Plus offers a quick order list natively. There is no native RFQ on any Shopify plan, including Shopify Plus. RFQ requires a third-party app such as SP Request a Quote, Quotes Guru, or a similar app from the Shopify App Store. This is a confirmed capability gap, not a minor limitation.

Step 4: Payment terms and checkout

WooCommerce with B2BKing supports custom payment terms including Net 30, Net 60, and custom intervals. Invoice generation happens without requiring upfront payment at checkout. Sub-accounts for team buyers are supported.

Buyers with approved terms can submit orders and receive invoices without a payment gateway transaction at the point of order.

Shopify Plus supports Net 15, Net 30, Net 60, and Net 90 terms natively, assignable per company with overdue tracking built into the platform. Purchase order number input is available at checkout on all paid Shopify plans.

Vaulted credit card storage for repeat buyers is a Plus-only feature. For most distributors transitioning from phone and email ordering, Shopify Plus's native net terms coverage is solid.

Key benefits of WooCommerce for B2B wholesale

WooCommerce's advantages for wholesale distributors come directly from its open-source architecture and the depth of its plugin ecosystem.

Full data ownership and PHP-level control

WooCommerce is self-hosted on your own infrastructure. You own the database, the customer records, and the order history. Every pricing rule, checkout flow, and order process is filterable through PHP hooks.

A developer can modify any part of the wholesale workflow without being limited by what a SaaS platform exposes through its API. For distributors with non-standard pricing logic, legacy ERP data structures, or complex approval workflows, this level of control is not optional. It's the reason WooCommerce is chosen over SaaS platforms for complex B2B builds.

Native RFQ through B2BKing

B2BKing's native RFQ system handles quote submission, negotiation, approval, and conversion to order within the WooCommerce admin. There's no additional app subscription, no third-party dependency, and no disconnect between the quote workflow and order data.

For distributors where a significant share of orders originate as quotes, this is a material operational advantage.

B2BKing has over 15,000 active installs and holds a 4.98 out of 5 star rating from more than 100 reviews on WordPress.org, with a last-updated date of May 2026.

MOQ enforcement at every level

WooCommerce enforces minimum order quantity at the product level, category level, and cart level through B2BKing. Rules are role-based, so different wholesale tiers can have different minimums on the same products.

Wholesale Suite, a complementary plugin from Rymera Web Co., also supports quantity-based and subtotal-based MOQ enforcement as an alternative configuration.

Lower annual software cost

WooCommerce itself is free and open-source. Wholesale Suite costs approximately $299 per year. B2BKing is a paid plugin (pricing is confirmed on the KingsPlugins website; specific tier pricing should be verified at purchase).

Managed WordPress hosting and development investment vary by complexity. The annual software licensing cost is substantially lower than Shopify Plus's $2,300 per month minimum.

Key benefits of Shopify Plus for B2B wholesale

Shopify Plus's advantages are strongest for distributors with standard wholesale workflows who prioritize managed infrastructure, fast deployment, and minimal ongoing developer involvement.

Managed infrastructure with no hosting overhead

Shopify Plus is a SaaS platform. Hosting, security patches, SSL, backups, and uptime are Shopify's responsibility. For a wholesale team without in-house WordPress developers, this removes a significant operational burden.

The $2,300 per month subscription covers infrastructure that a self-hosted WooCommerce store must manage separately.

Native company accounts, price lists, and net terms

Since Shopify expanded its B2B channel in late 2025, core wholesale features are now available on all paid Shopify plans. Company accounts, price lists, Net 15/30/60/90 payment terms, and purchase order support at checkout are all native. Standard plans allow up to three price lists; Plus allows unlimited.

For distributors whose wholesale needs fit within these standard parameters, Shopify's native B2B feature set covers a lot of ground without requiring plugin management or custom development for the core workflow.

Faster deployment for standard workflows

Shopify Plus's native, managed B2B features mean a wholesale store can reach production faster than a comparable WooCommerce build. WooCommerce builds require plugin configuration, custom registration forms, and developer-managed infrastructure. If your requirements are reasonably standard and your team is not technical, Shopify Plus is easier to operate and maintain long-term.

Shopify Flow automation without code

Shopify Flow lets your team build order routing, wholesale account approval, notification, and fulfillment automations without writing code. For distributors scaling up volume without scaling up dev headcount, this is a genuine operational advantage. Checkout extensibility through Shopify Functions is available on Plus if you need deeper workflow customization.

Common challenges and limitations

WooCommerce challenges for wholesale distributors

Hosting performance is entirely the store owner's responsibility. A high-volume wholesale catalog with complex pricing rules requires properly provisioned infrastructure and active monitoring. Managed WordPress hosts reduce this burden but add cost.

Plugin conflicts are a real operational risk when stacking multiple B2B plugins. B2BKing, Wholesale Suite, and the WooCommerce core extension can interact in unexpected ways without careful configuration and testing.

Developer dependency is ongoing for serious ERP integrations, custom checkout logic, and security maintenance. This is not a platform you can operate without a development relationship in place.

Shopify Plus challenges for wholesale distributors

The confirmed RFQ gap is the most significant operational limitation. If quoting is a core part of your sales process, you will need a third-party app on Shopify Plus. That introduces a cost dependency and a workflow disconnect that WooCommerce avoids through B2BKing's native RFQ system.

Tax exemption certificate collection and validation are not native. Shopify can mark a company location as tax-exempt, but certificate storage, validation, and audit-trail management require Avalara, ExemptSync, or TaxJar. If you manage dozens of exempt reseller accounts, budget for this from day one.

Pricing granularity is lower than WooCommerce at the individual user level. Per-user price overrides and role-based catalog visibility at the product attribute level may require third-party apps on Shopify. Shopify Plus also charges a 0.25% revenue share on gross merchandise value above $800,000 per month.

Real-world example

Consider a regional electrical components distributor with 800 SKUs, 120 wholesale accounts, and an existing ERP running on Epicor P21. They're building a wholesale portal and evaluating WooCommerce against Shopify Plus.

They have five hard requirements. The first three: customer-specific pricing per account, MOQ enforcement at the case level, and Net 30 terms for all wholesale accounts. The final two: RFQ capability for irregular orders, and real-time inventory sync with Epicor P21.

On WooCommerce with B2BKing, all five requirements are addressable within the platform and plugin stack. Customer-specific pricing is handled through B2BKing's user-level pricing rules. MOQ is enforced at cart checkout.

Net 30 is a native B2BKing payment term with invoice generation. RFQ is native. The only significant development investment is the Epicor P21 API connector, which requires a middleware-based custom integration.

On Shopify Plus, customer-specific pricing via price lists, Net 30 terms, and MOQ via quantity rules are all native. RFQ requires a third-party app. Epicor P21 integration requires a comparable middleware connector.

The Shopify Plus subscription at $2,300 per month adds a fixed infrastructure cost that the WooCommerce equivalent avoids.

For this distributor, the deciding factor typically comes down to RFQ volume. If 40% or more of orders originate as quotes, WooCommerce's native RFQ through B2BKing eliminates a third-party app dependency on Shopify. If the team is non-technical and wants managed infrastructure, Shopify Plus is easier to operate day to day.

Both platforms require custom development for a serious Epicor P21 integration.

Best practices

Define your RFQ volume before choosing a platform

If quoting is a core sales motion, count what percentage of your orders originate as quotes before choosing a platform. WooCommerce's native RFQ through B2BKing eliminates a third-party app dependency that Shopify Plus requires. If RFQ is a minor edge case for your business, this advantage shrinks considerably.

Map your ERP integration requirements early

Both platforms require custom development for serious distributor-grade ERP sync. Neither WooCommerce nor Shopify Plus offers a universal plug-and-play connector for systems like Epicor P21, NetSuite, or SAP Business One. Budget for middleware and API development as infrastructure-level investment, not a plugin purchase, before you commit to either platform.

Audit your wholesale pricing complexity

WooCommerce's plugin architecture is more granular than Shopify's price list system for user-level pricing and role-based catalog visibility. If your pricing model is relatively standard, Shopify Plus's native price lists cover the requirement without plugin management overhead.

Evaluate your team's technical capacity

Shopify Plus's managed infrastructure reduces developer overhead for day-to-day operations. WooCommerce requires ongoing developer involvement for hosting, plugin updates, security patches, and ERP integration maintenance. If your team doesn't have a WooCommerce development relationship in place, factor that into the real cost of choosing the open-source route.

Check your tax exemption volume

If you manage dozens of exempt reseller accounts, Avalara integration should be budgeted from day one on Shopify. On WooCommerce with B2BKing, certificate collection is integrated into the registration workflow, which reduces the operational burden. Either way, high-volume tax exemption management requires more than a native "exempt this account" flag.

Account for total cost of ownership, not just platform licensing

WooCommerce's lower software cost can be offset by higher development and hosting investment at the scale a serious wholesale distributor requires. Shopify Plus's $2,300 per month is predictable and includes infrastructure, but it's a floor, not a ceiling. Scope both options against your five-year transaction volume and workflow requirements before deciding on licensing cost alone.

Frequently asked questions

Does WooCommerce support B2B wholesale out of the box?

No. WooCommerce has no native B2B feature set. All wholesale functionality is added through plugins. B2BKing and Wholesale Suite are the two most widely used options.

B2BKing covers RFQ, MOQ, net terms, tiered pricing, and tax exemption in a single plugin. Wholesale Suite covers pricing, order forms, and lead capture through a modular bundle.

What is Shopify Plus B2B and how does it differ from standard Shopify?

Shopify launched a dedicated B2B sales channel on Shopify Plus in 2022. As of late 2025, foundational B2B features are available on all paid Shopify plans. These include company accounts, up to three price lists, net payment terms, and purchase order support.

Advanced features including unlimited price lists, a dedicated B2B storefront domain, vaulted credit card storage, and checkout extensibility via Shopify Functions remain Plus-only.

How does WooCommerce handle minimum order quantities (MOQ)?

WooCommerce enforces MOQ at the product level, category level, and cart level through B2BKing. Rules are role-based, so different wholesale tiers can have different minimums on the same products. Wholesale Suite also supports quantity-based and subtotal-based MOQ enforcement as an alternative or complementary configuration.

Can Shopify Plus do request for quote (RFQ)?

No, not natively. RFQ is not available on any Shopify plan, including Shopify Plus. A third-party app such as SP Request a Quote or Quotes Guru is required.

This adds a subscription cost and introduces a workflow dependency outside the native platform. WooCommerce with B2BKing handles RFQ natively, including quote negotiation, PDF export, and direct conversion to a live order.

Which platform handles net terms better, WooCommerce or Shopify?

Both handle net terms, but differently. Shopify Plus offers native Net 15, Net 30, Net 60, and Net 90 terms assignable per company with overdue tracking built in. This is a strong native offering that didn't exist at launch.

WooCommerce with B2BKing supports custom payment terms including Net 30, Net 60, and custom intervals with invoice generation. Note that net terms on WooCommerce are attributed to B2BKing specifically, not to Wholesale Suite.

How do I manage tax-exempt wholesale customers on each platform?

On WooCommerce with B2BKing, tax and VAT exemption is handled by role, and certificate collection is integrated into the registration workflow. On Shopify Plus, the "Don't collect tax" flag is available at the company location level natively.

Certificate collection, validation, and audit-trail management require Avalara, ExemptSync, or an equivalent service on Shopify, regardless of plan level.

Which platform integrates better with an ERP like NetSuite or Epicor?

Neither platform offers a universal native ERP connector for serious distributor-grade integration. Both require middleware or custom API development for real-time sync with Oracle NetSuite, Epicor P21, SAP Business One, or Microsoft Dynamics.

The infrastructure context differs: WooCommerce ERP connectors are self-managed, while Shopify's API-first architecture routes integrations through its managed infrastructure. Development cost is comparable for a serious integration on either platform.

What is the cost difference between WooCommerce and Shopify Plus for B2B?

Shopify Plus starts at $2,300 per month, or approximately $27,600 per year at minimum. WooCommerce is free and open-source; Wholesale Suite costs approximately $299 per year. B2BKing is a paid plugin with pricing available on the KingsPlugins website.

WooCommerce's annual software cost is lower, but managed WordPress hosting, development, and ongoing maintenance investment varies by complexity. Scope those costs against your specific requirements before drawing a direct comparison.

When should a distributor choose Shopify Plus over WooCommerce?

Choose Shopify Plus when your wholesale workflows are standard and your team needs managed infrastructure with fast deployment. It provides native company accounts, unlimited price lists, Net 15 through Net 90 terms with overdue tracking, and quantity rules without plugin dependencies.

The tradeoff is a $2,300 per month minimum subscription and a third-party app dependency for RFQ. Pricing control at the individual user level is less granular than WooCommerce with B2BKing.

Conclusion

For wholesale distributors with complex, non-standard workflows, WooCommerce is the stronger platform. It handles RFQ, custom net terms, PHP-level pricing flexibility, and ERP-dependent data flows within the platform and plugin stack through B2BKing. Developer-level access to every pricing and checkout touchpoint is the key advantage.

For B2B operations with standard wholesale workflows, Shopify Plus is a viable and competitive choice. Native company accounts, price lists, and net terms cover the core requirements without plugin management. Managed infrastructure and faster deployment reduce ongoing developer overhead.

The decision isn't about which platform is better in the abstract. It's about which platform fits your specific workflow requirements, your team's technical capacity, and your five-year volume trajectory.

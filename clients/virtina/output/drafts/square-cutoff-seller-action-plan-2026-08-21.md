## [H2: What did Square actually tell hemp and CBD sellers?]
(anchor id: what-square-told-sellers)

Square told sellers that CBD and hemp products can no longer be sold through Square, in stores or online. The notice reached affected accounts around August 7, 2026. It arrived by email and it named a product category, not a particular business.

The wording was plain. Selling these products "online or in person, will no longer be permitted."

That line comes from the seller notice, as reported by merchants who received it. Square has not published a policy page saying the same thing.

This was a commercial decision about a whole category. Nobody at Square reviewed your refund rate, your paperwork, or your lab results and found a problem.

None of this is your fault. Square is moving ahead of a change in federal law that narrows what counts as hemp. That law matters more than the notice does.

## [H2: Which Square deadline applies to you, October 15 or November 5?]
(anchor id: which-deadline-applies)

Your deadline is October 15 for a mixed catalog, or November 5 if hemp is your main business. Square sent different instructions to the two groups. Work out which group you are in before you do anything else.

### [H3: October 15, 2026, if CBD is one part of a wider catalog]

If CBD sits alongside other products, those items have to be gone by October 15, 2026. That means every CBD, hemp, and hemp-derived item, in person and online.

A hemp-derived cannabinoid is any compound taken from the hemp plant, including CBD, delta-8, and THCA. If it came from hemp, it comes out.

The rest of the account stays active. Your non-hemp sales carry on, your card reader keeps working, and your payouts continue.

### [H3: November 5, 2026, if hemp is your main business]

If hemp and CBD are most of what you sell, the entire Square account closes on November 5, 2026. The cutoff is 11:59pm EST.

Not the listings. The account. That covers the card reader, the online store, the customer directory, and the payouts.

The next subsection gives you the test for working out which group you are in. Run it today, not in October.

One honest caveat. Reported closure dates have varied between merchants, so treat November 5 as the outside edge.

### [H3: How do you work out which group you are in?]

Pull last quarter's sales by item and work out what share came from CBD, hemp, and hemp-derived SKUs. If those items are most of your revenue and most of your catalog, plan for the November 5 outcome.

Your Square transaction report gives you that number in twenty minutes. Check the notice you received too, because it names your category.

If you are on the line between the two groups, plan for the worse one. The work is identical either way. ## [H2: Why is Square doing this now?]
(anchor id: why-now-hemp-law)

Square is acting ahead of a new federal law that narrows what legally counts as hemp. The law passed nine months ago.

### [H3: What Section 781 changed about the word hemp]

The provision is Section 781 of H.R. 5371, the act that ended the shutdown. It was signed on November 12, 2025.

The 2018 Farm Bill measured delta-9 THC only, at or below 0.3% dry weight. That single measurement created the delta-8 and THCA market.

It also opened the [INTERNAL LINK: CBD ecommerce market | purpose: link to https://virtina.com/cbd-ecommerce-how-to-make-the-most-out-of-the-young-market/, natural where the piece explains how the 2018 rule opened the category to small sellers] to thousands of small sellers. THCA is the acid form of THC that converts to THC when heated.

Section 781 now counts THCA, so the plant standard becomes total THC at 0.3% dry weight. It also excludes cannabinoids synthesized outside the plant, which covers CBD-to-delta-8 conversion.

Industrial hemp is preserved: fiber, stalk, grain, seed oil, and microgreens.

### [H3: Why 0.4 milligrams per container is the number that matters]

Finished consumer products are capped at 0.4 mg of total THC, including THCA, per container. This is the number that decides whether a product on your shelf survives.

Read it carefully. It is milligrams per container, not a percentage.

Several articles state it as "0.3% total THC." That produces a completely different answer for a gummy or a beverage.

Container means the innermost packaging that encloses the final product for retail sale. That is the bottle, the bag, the box, the can, or the cartridge.

### [H3: Is the date moving? What Congress did in August]

Section 781 was enacted on November 12, 2025 and scheduled to take effect on November 12, 2026. That date is now contested, and as of late August 2026 it is not settled.

On August 8, 2026 the Senate passed H.R. 6500 by 90 votes to 6. That bill proposes a delay to December 11, 2026. On August 9 it voted 61 to 32 to table an amendment keeping the original date.

None of that is law. The House votes in September, and it previously passed a version without the hemp language.

### [H3: Does a federal delay reopen your Square account?]

No. Square set its own commercial dates, and a change in federal law does not change a private company's dates.

The December 11 headlines are being read as a reprieve. They are not one for your Square account. Both Square dates stand regardless of what the House does.

## [H2: Which of your products still count as hemp under the new rule?]
(anchor id: which-products-count-as-hemp)

A finished product still counts as hemp only if its container holds no more than 0.4 mg of total THC. That figure includes THCA and it is measured per container, not per serving.

Moving your store does not change that number. A 5 mg gummy is a 5 mg gummy on any platform and with any processor. No migration makes it legal.

That is why this is the first decision rather than the last one. It costs an afternoon and no money. Making it now stops you paying for a fix that cannot help.

### [H3: How do you sort your catalog into two lists?]

Work through every SKU once, in this order:

[BULLET LIST - Template F]
- **Pull the COA.** Get the current certificate of analysis for every SKU. A COA is the lab report that comes with each batch.
- **Read total THC.** Use the total THC figure including THCA, not the delta-9 line alone.
- **Convert to milligrams.** Work out milligrams per innermost container, not per serving and not as a percentage.
- **Flag the synthetics.** Mark anything with a cannabinoid made outside the plant.
- **Separate industrial hemp.** Put fiber, seed oil, and grain lines in their own bucket.

Two lists come out of that afternoon. One survives Section 781. One does not.

## [H2: What happens to the money Square is still holding?]
(anchor id: what-happens-to-your-money)

Square can hold part of your money after the account closes, and its own terms say so plainly. This is not a rumor. It is written into the agreement you accepted at signup.

### [H3: Reserves and delayed payouts, in Square's own words]

A reserve is money the processor keeps back to cover refunds and disputes that have not happened yet. Square's [EXTERNAL LINK: Payment Terms | target: https://squareup.com/us/en/legal/general/payment | attributes: target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"] allow it to hold funds this way, in section 14.

The reserve can be raised, reduced, or removed at any time, at Square's sole discretion. Section 12 lets Square defer a payout or restrict access to your proceeds.

That can be temporary or indefinite. At closure, funds held in custody are paid out on your existing payout schedule.

### [H3: The 120-day tail on refunds and chargebacks]

Square documents that refunds can be processed up to 120 days from the day you accepted the payment. A sale you made in September can still come back at you in January.

That is long after the store is gone. It is also the whole reason a processor holds money past a closing date.

A chargeback is a customer asking their bank to reverse a payment. Liability stays with you, and pending chargebacks can delay payouts.

### [H3: Is the 90-day hold real?]

No. The widely repeated "90-day hold" on Square funds appears nowhere in Square's terms. The same goes for the 180-day version.

It circulates through blogs published by firms selling competing merchant accounts. The documented number is the 120-day refund window above.

If you are shopping for [INTERNAL LINK: high-risk payment processing | purpose: link to https://virtina.com/high-risk-payment-processing-ecommerce-businesses/, natural where the piece warns that processor-published blogs are the source of the 90-day myth], expect content written by people who want your account.

## [H2: What can you get out of Square before the account closes?]
(anchor id: what-data-you-can-export)

You can export your item library, transactions, and customer list yourself, but saved cards need a phone call. The self-serve exports take half an hour. The card export takes up to two weeks.

### [H3: The exports you can run yourself today]

Do these this week, not on deadline day. Keep a copy off the machine you run the store on.

[BULLET LIST - Template F]
- **Item library.** Dashboard, then Items and services, Items, Item library, Actions, Export Library. Square recommends .xlsx.
- **Transactions.** Reports, Transactions, set the date range, then Export and Transactions CSV. Customer names come with it.
- **Customer directory.** Export it from the Dashboard alongside your 1099-K forms.

That catalog export is what the new store gets imported from. A clean file now saves rebuilding work later.

### [H3: Saved cards, the one export with a two-week clock]

Cards on file are card numbers stored with the processor so repeat customers do not re-enter them. Square will move them, but the process is narrow.

Only the account owner can request it, and it is done by phone, not from the Dashboard. Square's own [EXTERNAL LINK: card on file export guide | target: https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/7871-export-card-on-file-to-third-party-payment-processors | attributes: target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"] sets out the rest.

Square only sends card data to a PCI DSS Level 1 compliant processor, the highest tier of card-security certification. The average export takes up to two weeks.

Read that last part again, because the sequence catches people out. You have to be approved by the new processor before Square will release the cards.

The processor decision is not the last step in the move. It is the gate on rescuing every saved card you have.

Here is what that looks like in practice. A hemp beverage seller with 400 subscription customers calls Square on November 3 to move the cards. The export needs a receiving processor already approved, so those 400 customers have to re-enter their details.

### [H3: What does not come with you]

Reviews, loyalty balances, gift card liabilities, booking history, and marketing lists do not transfer. Budget for rebuilding each one.

A Square Online site is not portable either. The storefront gets rebuilt on the new platform rather than moved.

## [H2: Why could Square do this at all?]
(anchor id: why-square-could-do-this)

Square could do this because you were never its merchant, you were a sub-merchant on Square's merchant ID. That single fact explains both how easy signup was and how fast this ended.

### [H3: What a payment facilitator actually is]

Square is a payment facilitator, or PayFac. One company holds the merchant account with the bank, and many small sellers process underneath it.

You are a sub-merchant on that account, and Square is the merchant of record. That is why signup took ten minutes with no underwriting and no credit check.

It is also why the relationship can end unilaterally. Square's General Terms allow it to suspend or terminate an account at any time, for any reason.

Every hosted platform carries this exposure, as the [INTERNAL LINK: vape retailers on Shopify | purpose: link to https://virtina.com/shopify-vape-store-woocommerce-migration/, one sentence only on platform risk per the uniqueness fence, then move on] found last year.

### [H3: What owning your own merchant ID changes]

A dedicated high-risk merchant account gives you your own merchant ID, or MID. It also gives you a direct relationship with an acquiring bank.

An acquiring bank is the bank that holds the account and settles your card money. Getting one requires underwriting, which is the slow part and also the point.

A bank that has read your COAs, your statements, and your formation documents knows your business. It does not drop you because a category got reclassified.

Applications should start at least six weeks ahead of go-live. From late August, that window is already tight.

We are not going to name processors, because every firm publishing on this topic is selling accounts. Ask for four things instead: your own MID, interchange-plus pricing, written reserve terms, and hemp underwriting on the record. Our notes on [INTERNAL LINK: payment gateway providers | purpose: link to https://virtina.com/payment-gateway-service-providers/, natural where the piece lists what to ask a processor for] cover how the gateway and the account fit together.

### [H3: Where the store itself should live]

The narrow point here is what self-hosted WooCommerce lets you attach and what it lets you keep. You can connect any third-party gateway, including one running on your own MID.

Products, customers, and order history sit in a database on infrastructure you control. The next move is yours to make, not somebody else's to approve.

Two honest caveats. WooPayments, Automattic's own gateway, restricts CBD. "WooCommerce is open" is true of the plugin, not of Automattic's payment product.

Self-hosting also moves responsibility to you: hosting, security, PCI scope, updates, and your host's acceptable use policy.

That is a real cost, not a free win. ## [H2: People also ask]
(anchor id: people-also-ask)

### [H3: Can I keep selling CBD on Square after October 15, 2026?]

No. Square's notice requires all CBD, hemp, and hemp-derived items to be removed from the catalog, in person and online. Sellers whose business is primarily hemp face a full account closure on November 5 instead.

### [H3: Does the proposed federal delay change Square's deadline?]

No. Square set its own commercial dates and they do not move with the law. As of late August 2026 the proposed delay is not law: it passed the Senate but not the House.

### [H3: Will Square hold my money after my account closes?]

It can. Square's Payment Terms allow reserves and deferred payouts, at Square's discretion. Refunds can also be processed up to 120 days after the original payment, so a balance can sit unpaid past a closing date.

### [H3: Can I take my saved customer cards with me?]

Yes, with conditions. Square only sends card data to a PCI DSS Level 1 compliant processor, and only the account owner can request it by phone. The average export takes up to two weeks, so the new processor has to be approved first.

## [H2: Conclusion]
(anchor id: conclusion)

You are dealing with two problems that look like one. The infrastructure problem is fixable: own your merchant ID, own your database, and stop processing under somebody else's account. The catalog problem is not fixable by any platform, any processor, or any agency, including us.

So sort the catalog this week, before you spend a dollar on anything else. Then work backwards from your date, which is either October 15 or November 5, 2026.

## [H2: Frequently asked questions]
(anchor id: faq)

### [FAQ Q1: How do I know if Square counts my business as hemp-primary?]

Check the notice Square sent you first, because it names your category. If it does not, pull last quarter's sales by item and work out what share of revenue came from hemp SKUs. If those items are most of your revenue and most of your catalog, plan for the November 5 closure.

### [FAQ Q2: What does "total THC" mean, and why does THCA count now?]

Total THC measures all the THC a product can produce, not just the THC already present. THCA is the acid form that converts to THC when heated, so a delta-9 test alone misses it. The 2018 Farm Bill measured delta-9 only, which created the THCA market. Section 781 measures total THC including THCA, at not more than 0.3% dry weight.

### [FAQ Q3: What does 0.4 mg per container mean for a ten-pack of gummies?]

It means the whole ten-pack has to contain no more than 0.4 mg of total THC, not each gummy. Section 781 defines container as the innermost packaging that encloses the final product for retail sale. Reading the cap per serving instead of per container puts SKUs on the wrong list.

### [FAQ Q4: Is the 90-day hold on Square funds real?]

It is not in Square's terms, and neither is the 180-day version. Both figures circulate through blogs published by firms selling competing merchant accounts. The documented number is different: Square states that refunds can be processed up to 120 days from the payment date.

### [FAQ Q5: How long does a high-risk merchant account take to approve?]

Plan on about six weeks from application to live, and start earlier if you can. Underwriting is what takes the time. It typically asks for certificates of analysis, processing statements, formation documents, and sometimes a state license.

### [FAQ Q6: Can I export my Square item library and customer list myself?]

Yes. The item library exports from Dashboard, then Items and services, Items, Item library, Actions, Export Library, as .xlsx or CSV. Transactions export from Reports with a date range you set, and the customer directory exports from the Dashboard. Saved cards on file are the exception and cannot be exported this way.

### [FAQ Q7: What happens to an outstanding Square Loan if my account closes?]

An account closure affects the repayment terms, because repayment normally comes out of your Square sales. Reports that Square offered alternative arrangements to affected hemp sellers could not be confirmed. Contact Square in writing, ask how repayment works after closure, and keep the reply.

### [FAQ Q8: If Congress delays the hemp law, can I stay on Square?]

No. Square made a commercial decision and set its own dates, and those dates do not move with the legislation. As of late August 2026 the Senate has passed a proposed delay to December 11, 2026, but the House has not voted. Either way, both Square dates stand.

## Audience

A small business owner who sells CBD or hemp through Square, often as both POS and online store. Revenue is small enough that Square's all-in-one convenience was the right call in 2019 and large enough that losing it stops the income.

**What they already know:** their products, their margins, their customers. They know what a chargeback is. They have heard of "high risk" processing and assume it means expensive.

**What they do not know:** what a payment facilitator is, that they are a sub-merchant on somebody else's merchant ID, that "total THC" now includes THCA, that the finished-product cap is measured in milligrams per container rather than as a percentage, and that saved customer cards cannot be exported without a phone call and a two-week wait.

**What they are trying to figure out, in this order:** Is my account closing, or just my CBD listings? When exactly? Does the money I am owed still come? Can I take my customers with me? What do I do first?

Note the split from Virtina's usual reader. This is not a VP of eCommerce. Assume commercial literacy, not platform literacy. Every payments or legal term gets defined in the same sentence it appears in, in plain words, and then gets used normally.

## THE GOVERNING CONSTRAINT: "simple but user should feel"

This is not a tone note. It is the brief's first requirement and it outranks everything below it except factual accuracy.

### The register

Level, plain, and unhurried. The voice of someone who has read the notice carefully and is sitting across the kitchen table with it. Short words over long ones. Concrete nouns over abstractions. Numbers over adjectives.

The emotional weight comes from **specifics**, never from intensity. "Your whole Square account closes at 11:59pm on November 5" carries more feeling than any adjective the creator could reach for. Say the fact, then stop. Do not decorate it.

### The five moments where the reader must feel understood

Use these five and no more. A sixth turns the post from steady into sentimental.

1. **Introduction, second paragraph.** Name the concrete shape of their day. They found out by email, on a Friday in August, from a company they have never once spoken to on the phone. One sentence. No adjectives. Then move.
2. **End of section 1.** The permission line: *"None of this is your fault."* Say it exactly once in the article and never again. Follow it immediately with a fact, not with comfort.
3. **Section 2, the November 5 subsection.** The hardest sentence in the piece, delivered flat: for hemp-primary sellers the entire account closes, not just the CBD listings. Then, within two sentences, give the test that tells them which group they are in. Fear must never sit on the page for longer than two sentences without an action attached.
4. **Section 4 opening.** The concession, said to their face: moving your store does not make a 5 mg gummy legal. Immediately follow with why that is good news today: this decision is free, it costs one afternoon, and it stops them spending money on the wrong fix.
5. **Section 8, step 1.** *"Do not buy anything this week."* This is the article's payoff. Relief delivered as permission not to spend, earned by seven sections of honesty.

### Banned, because they cheapen it

No "nightmare", "devastating", "livelihood", "heartbreaking", "your worst fear", "overnight" (also factually wrong, notice was about ten weeks), "imagine if", "picture this", "we know how you feel", "don't panic". No stacked rhetorical questions. No exclamation marks. No reassurance that is not backed by a fact in the same paragraph.

### H2 3 — Why is Square doing this now?
`id="why-now-hemp-law"` · ~340 words

**First sentence (direct answer, locked):** "Square is acting ahead of a new federal law that narrows what legally counts as hemp."

**H3: What Section 781 changed about the word hemp**
- Vehicle: **Section 781 of H.R. 5371**, the appropriations act that ended the shutdown. **Signed November 12, 2025.**
- The 2018 Farm Bill measured **delta-9 THC only**, at or below 0.3% dry weight. That single measurement is what created the delta-8 and THCA market.
- Section 781 moves the plant standard to **total THC, explicitly including THCA**, at not more than 0.3% dry weight.
- It excludes cannabinoids synthesized or manufactured outside the plant, which covers CBD-to-delta-8 conversion.
- It preserves **industrial hemp**: fiber, stalk, grain, seed oil, microgreens, qualifying research.

**H3: Why 0.4 milligrams per container is the number that matters**
- Finished consumer products are capped at **0.4 mg of total THC, including THCA, per container**.
- **Milligrams per container, not a percentage.** This is the single most misread number in the topic, and a ranking competitor states it as "0.3% total THC," which produces a completely different answer for a gummy or a beverage.
- "Container" means **the innermost packaging that encloses the final product for retail sale**: the bottle, bag, box, can, or cartridge.

**H3: Is the date moving? What Congress did in August**
- **FACT DISCIPLINE, NON-NEGOTIABLE. Never state a flat effective date.** Write it as contested and time-stamped.
- Required framing: enacted November 12, 2025. Scheduled to take effect **November 12, 2026**. On **August 8, 2026** the Senate passed **H.R. 6500** by **90 to 6**, proposing a delay to **December 11, 2026**. On August 9 the Senate voted 61 to 32 to table an amendment that would have kept the original date.
- **It is not law.** The House votes in September.
- Timestamp the paragraph explicitly: **"as of late August 2026."**
- The reported carve-out (delay applies to naturally derived products, synthetics stay on the November date) is **single-sourced**. Either omit it or attribute it as reported and unconfirmed. Do not state it as settled.
- The post must read correctly whichever way the House votes.

**H3: Does a federal delay reopen your Square account?**
- **No.** One-word answer, then the reason: Square set its own commercial deadline. A change in the law does not change a private company's dates.
- This is a field-wide gap. Merchants are reading the December 11 news as a reprieve. It is not one, for their Square account.

### H2 5 — What happens to the money Square is still holding?
`id="what-happens-to-your-money"` · ~300 words

**First sentence (direct answer, locked):** "Square can hold part of your money after the account closes, and its own terms say so plainly."

**H3: Reserves and delayed payouts, in Square's own words**
- Square may withhold funds by suspending or delaying payouts, and may require funds be held in a separate reserve account. The reserve may be raised, reduced, or removed at any time, at Square's sole discretion (Payment Terms, sec. 14).
- Square may defer payout or restrict access to proceeds, temporarily or indefinitely, pending investigation or dispute (sec. 12).
- After closure, funds held in custody are paid out according to your payout schedule, subject to the other conditions (sec. 38).
- Paraphrase throughout. Keep any quoted fragment under 15 words.

**H3: The 120-day tail on refunds and chargebacks**
- Square documents that refunds can be processed **up to 120 days from the day you accepted the payment**.
- Explain what that means in plain terms: a sale you made in September can still come back at you in January, after the store is gone. That is why a processor holds money past a closing date.
- Chargeback liability stays with the seller, and pending chargebacks can delay payouts.

**H3: Is the 90-day hold real?**
- **No, and this matters.** The widely repeated "90-day hold" appears nowhere in Square's terms. It circulates only through blogs published by processors selling competing merchant accounts.
- **Do not use the 90-day figure as fact anywhere in the post.** Naming it as folklore and correcting it is a credibility win. Stating it is a factual error.
- Same treatment for "180-day hold."
- Square Loans: if the account closes with a balance, repayment terms are affected. Reports of alternative arrangements are unverified. Tell the reader to ask Square in writing and keep the reply.

### H2 6 — What can you get out of Square before the account closes?
`id="what-data-you-can-export"` · ~340 words

**First sentence (direct answer, locked):** "You can export your item library, transactions, and customer list yourself, but saved cards need a phone call."

**H3: The exports you can run yourself today**
- Item library: Dashboard, then Items & services, Items, Item library, Actions, **Export Library**. Square recommends .xlsx; CSV also works.
- Transactions: Reports, Transactions, set the date range, **Export, Transactions CSV**. Includes customer names attached to purchases.
- Customer directory and 1099-K forms export from the Dashboard.
- Tell them to do this **this week**, not on deadline day, and to keep a copy off the machine they run the store on.

**H3: Saved cards, the one export with a two-week clock**
- **This is the post's strongest under-covered fact. Give it room.**
- Only the **account owner** can request it.
- Square only sends to a **PCI DSS Level 1-compliant payment processor**. PCI DSS Level 1 means the highest tier of card-security certification. Define it in that clause and move on.
- The request is made **by phone** to Square Support for ownership verification. There is no dashboard button.
- **The average card export timeframe is up to two weeks.**
- Square sends an encrypted file directly to the new processor.
- **The sequencing insight, state it explicitly and in its own short paragraph:** you have to choose the new processor before Square will release the cards. That reverses the order most people assume. The processor decision is not the last step, it is the gate on the card rescue.

**Case snippet (mandatory element for problem/solution, 3 sentences, goes here):**
Frame as a hypothetical, never as a Virtina client, and never with a named brand. Direction: a hemp beverage seller with 400 customers on monthly subscriptions calls Square on November 3 to move the saved cards. The export takes up to two weeks and needs a receiving processor already approved. Those 400 customers now have to enter their card details again, and some of them will not.

**H3: What does not come with you**
- Reviews, loyalty balances, gift card liabilities, appointment and booking history, Square-native marketing lists.
- A Square Online site is **not portable**. The storefront gets rebuilt, not moved. Say it once, without drama.

### H2 7 — Why could Square do this at all?
`id="why-square-could-do-this"` · ~330 words

**First sentence (direct answer, locked):** "Square could do this because you were never its merchant, you were a sub-merchant on Square's merchant ID."

**H3: What a payment facilitator actually is**
- Square is a **payment facilitator**, or PayFac. Define it in the same sentence: one company holds the merchant account with the bank and lets many small sellers process underneath it.
- You are a **sub-merchant**. Square is the **merchant of record**.
- That is why signup took ten minutes with no underwriting and no credit check. It is the same reason the relationship can end unilaterally and fast.
- Square's General Terms allow it to suspend or terminate an account **at any time for any reason**. Put that fact here, not in section 1.

**H3: What owning your own merchant ID changes**
- A **dedicated high-risk merchant account** gives you your **own merchant ID (MID)** and a direct relationship with an **acquiring bank**, after **underwriting**.
- Underwriting is the slow part and the point. A bank that has read your COAs, your processing statements, and your formation documents does not drop you because a category got reclassified in a blog post.
- Underwriting typically asks for COAs, processing statements, business formation documents, product and lab documentation, and sometimes a state licence. Applications should start **at least six weeks** ahead. From today that window is already tight.
- **Do not name specific high-risk processors.** Every one of them is publishing on this keyword to sell accounts. Describe the category and what to ask for: own MID, interchange-plus pricing, written reserve terms, hemp underwriting on the record.

**H3: Where the store itself should live**
- **One sentence only on platform risk, then link and move on.** The vape retailers on Shopify story already made this argument in full; do not rebuild it.
- The point here is narrower and different: self-hosted WooCommerce lets you attach **any** third-party gateway, including one with your own MID, and it keeps products, customers, and order history in a database you control.
- **Two honest caveats, both mandatory:**
  - **WooPayments, Automattic's own gateway, restricts CBD.** "WooCommerce is open" is true of the plugin, not of Automattic's payment product. Self-hosting is the part that matters.
  - Self-hosting moves responsibility to you: hosting, security, PCI scope, updates, and your host's own acceptable use policy. A real trade-off, not a free win.

### H2 8 — What is the right order to do this in?
`id="what-order-to-do-this-in"` · ~380 words · **MANDATORY NUMBERED STEPS + CHECKLIST**

**First sentence (direct answer, locked):** "Sort your catalog first, choose a processor second, and move the store third."

Explain the ordering rule in one line before the steps: every step below exists because the step after it cannot start until it is done.

**H3: Week 1, decisions and exports, not purchases**
1. **Do not buy anything this week.** (**Feel moment 5.** This is the payoff line. Let it stand as its own short sentence.)
2. Sort every SKU into "survives Section 781" and "does not," using total THC per container.
3. Export your item library, transactions, and customer directory. Save a copy off-site.

5. Apply for a dedicated high-risk merchant account with your own MID. Have COAs, processing statements, and formation documents ready before you start. Allow about six weeks.

**H3: Weeks 5 to 6, cutover and the tail**
8. Test checkout on the new store with real cards before you switch anything off.
9. Email your customers before Square does. Tell them the store is moving and why. A subscriber who hears it from you re-enters a card. One who hears it from a failed charge does not.
10. Keep the Square account open, with CBD items removed, until reserves and the 120-day refund window have run down. Do not close it in a hurry.

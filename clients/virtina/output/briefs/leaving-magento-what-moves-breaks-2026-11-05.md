# Brief: Magento to WooCommerce migration: what moves, what breaks, and when to stay

## 1. Thesis

> Leaving Magento for WooCommerce is a data-mapping job, and the parts that do not map (company accounts, shared catalogs, quotes, store views, custom modules, password hashes, URL structure) decide whether you should go at all.

**Why this angle**
- No Virtina asset maps Magento objects to WooCommerce objects. 42582 and 43110 cover whether to leave, 29601 is a four-step tool click-through, 43054 is the same idea for a Shopify destination, 37776 and 10658 are feature comparisons.
- The research found no competitor (five assessed) that offers all of: an object-by-object status map, a sourced when-not-to-move test, the password truth with the vendor conflict stated, URL mapping tied to Google's own redirect rules, and a neutral route table.
- It is a decision-shaped post (the AI-visibility preference in memory): a mapping table, a route table and a scorecard are lift-ready for a model.

**Why now.** Trend signal is moderate and low volume: "magento to woocommerce migration" is the top related query under both "woocommerce migration" and "magento migration" and is rising under the latter (scan of 2026-11-05). Do not call it "Trends-proven" or cite any index figure in the post. Adobe's own pages (export, B2B, versions) were updated in October 2026, so every fact can carry a date.

**Why Virtina.** Owned themes 2 (replatforming without breaking what works) and 5 (platform selection), plus theme 1 (B2B). Strongest play: migration. Honest stay cases are brand-correct (no blanket platform dismissals). The service answer is the WooCommerce migration services page and WooCommerce development.

VP eCommerce, Director of Digital, ecommerce manager or founder on Magento or Adobe Commerce, $1M to $500M revenue. They know their catalog, ERP, customer groups and traffic. ### H2 1 (about 560 words), id `what-moves-cleanly`
**What moves cleanly from Magento to WooCommerce?**
First sentence (locked): "Simple, virtual, downloadable and grouped products, plus categories, tags and images, move cleanly through WooCommerce's core product importer, while customer and order records move with an extension or tool."

  Status vocabulary (exactly four): Moves, Moves with cleanup, Rebuild, Breaks unless handled. Use "Check first" only for the payment-token row.
  Rows (16, wording sourced as listed in section 8):
  1. Simple, virtual and downloadable products | Same core types | Moves | Export CSV, import with the core importer.
  2. Grouped products | Grouped products | Moves | Match children by SKU.
  3. Configurable products | Variable products and variations | Moves with cleanup | Rebuild attribute columns and parent SKU on variation rows.
  4. Bundle products | No core type; needs a bundles extension | Rebuild | Choose the extension before import.
  5. Categories, tags, images | Importer takes category paths and image URLs | Moves with cleanup | Add alt text afterwards.
  6. Customers and addresses | Not in core; an official import-export extension or tool | Moves with a tool | Plan the password step (row 8).
  7. Orders and history | Extension or tool; HPOS affects plugin support | Moves with a tool | Test on staging with HPOS on.
  8. Password hashes | Magento format is not a WordPress format | Breaks unless handled | Plugin check at login, or customers reset.
  9. Customer groups | WordPress user roles plus a pricing plugin | Rebuild | Recreate groups, then prices.
  10. Tier and advanced pricing | No core equivalent documented | Rebuild | Pricing plugin or custom.
  11. Tax rules | Tax classes and rate tables | Rebuild | Re-enter or import rate CSV; handle tax-exempt buyers separately.

### H2 3 (about 480 words), id `what-breaks-on-launch-day`
**What breaks on launch day if you do nothing?**
First sentence (locked): "Customer logins, product URLs and saved payment methods break on launch day unless you plan for each one."
- **H3: Why do customer passwords stop working?** Locked wording: "WordPress cannot verify Magento password hashes natively; a plugin can verify them at login, or customers reset." Support: Adobe's documentation describes hashes stored as hash, salt and version, with Argon 2ID13 as the default and SHA256 otherwise; WordPress core checks its own formats (WordPress 6.8 moved to bcrypt, older phpass hashes keep working and are rehashed at next login). Vendors disagree on retention: one says passwords can be kept for supported Magento formats via its plugin, another only for supported formats and otherwise a reset flow. Decision for the reader: choose plugin verification at login or a planned reset email, and test both on staging. Do NOT say hashes "cannot be exported". Do NOT say whether the Magento export includes the hash column (unverified). Do not repeat 43054's "can't be exported" wording.
- **H3: What happens to URLs and rankings?** Adobe: product URL suffix is configurable (html or htm) and category path is not included in product URLs by default (do NOT state .html is the default). WooCommerce defaults: product base `product`, category base `product-category`; permalink options are Default, Shop base, Shop base with category, Custom base. Google (one external link, anchor "Google site-move guidance", 3 words): server-side 301 or 308, map each old URL to its equivalent, do not send many URLs to an irrelevant page such as the homepage, avoid chains (ideally no more than 3 hops), Change of Address is for domain moves only, keep redirects at least a year, expect temporary fluctuation (medium sites may take weeks or more). Internal link: "rankings after a redesign" (43029). One tool lists redirects as a premium feature and the official extension lists none; say "check this before you buy".
- **H3: Can saved payment methods move?** Careful wording only: whether saved payment methods carry over depends on the payment processor and the gateway used on WooCommerce. Ask the processor before launch and plan for customers re-entering a card if the answer is no. Do not state that tokens "are held by the gateway" as a fact.

### H2 5 (about 560 words), id `when-not-to-move`
**When should you not move from Magento to WooCommerce?**
First sentence (locked): "Do not move from Magento to WooCommerce while your store depends on features that WooCommerce's core does not list, such as company accounts, shared catalogs and negotiable quotes."
- Open with one sentence handing the generic upgrade-or-move question to the sibling posts: internal links "Magento version support cliff" (42582) and "Magento 2.4.6 end of support" (43110). Do NOT restate 42582's five store profiles or its "most leavers land on WooCommerce" line.
- Frame each H3 as a mapping gap, not a store profile:
- **H3: Do company accounts and quotes map to WooCommerce?** Adobe's B2B documentation lists company accounts, company credit, company hierarchy, shared catalogs, quick order, negotiable quotes, purchase order approvals and requisition lists; shared catalogs and negotiable quotes apply to stores configured for company accounts (do NOT state which edition has them). WooCommerce's core documentation does not list these features; they come from B2B extensions, so costing the move means costing the extensions and the testing. Internal links: "Adobe Commerce B2B features" (38095) and nothing else here.
- **H3: Do multi-store hierarchies carry over?** Adobe's hierarchy is global, website, store and store view; stores share one catalog and one admin, store views carry language and currency symbol. WordPress Multisite gives each site its own content tables and shares only the user table. Label the conclusion as inference from the two documentation sets: a different model, so rebuild, not migrate.
- **H3: What if custom modules and ERP links run the business?** Custom modules have no migration path; an ERP or OMS connector built against Magento has to be rebuilt or replaced. Internal link: "WooCommerce ERP integration" (42108).
- **H3: Is a peak-season cutover worth the risk?** Fresh, short, practical. Google says expect temporary fluctuation after a move; do not cut over inside your peak selling weeks.
- **Scorecard** (Template N, caption "Stay-or-go scorecard, Virtina's judgment, not a sourced benchmark"). Eight yes/no tests, 2 columns (Test | Why it matters), then a verdict block as 3 Template F bullets:
  1. Buyers need negotiable quotes or approval chains inside the storefront.
  2. Prices differ by company account or shared catalog, not just by customer group.
  3. You run several stores or store views with shared catalog but different languages or currencies.
  4. Custom modules handle pricing, checkout or fulfillment logic you cannot describe on one page.
  5. An ERP, PIM or OMS connector was custom-built against Magento.
  6. A large share of your search traffic lands on URLs you cannot map one to one.
  7. The cutover would land inside your peak selling weeks.
  8. No one on your team can own plugin updates, hosting and staging after the move.

# Brief: When Volusion stops growing with you

## Thesis

> If Volusion keeps billing you without warning and blocking the integrations you need, the question is no longer whether to migrate — it is whether you can afford to wait any longer.

## Why this, why now, why us

- **Why this angle:** Every competing article approaches this topic from the outside, explaining Volusion vs. WooCommerce as a neutral comparison. This piece starts where the merchant already is: inside a frustrating situation they recognise as unsustainable. The "you are not crazy for wanting to leave" framing does not exist anywhere in the current search results. That gap is the angle.
- **Why now:** Volusion has lost 75% of its active stores since 2020, declining from 13,889 to 3,526 as of May 2026, with a 20-to-1 defection ratio in the last 90 days. Merchants still on the platform are the late movers. This moment — when network effects are reversing and platform investment is clearly slowing — is the urgency.
- **Why Virtina:** Virtina is a certified Volusion Partner and WooCommerce Expert. They have done both sides of this equation. The piece should carry quiet authority on the migration steps without ever making Virtina the subject.

## Audience

Volusion merchants at $50K–$500K annual GMV. They run B2C stores or early-stage B2B wholesale. They know their Volusion admin cold. They have hit at least one of these walls: a surprise billing jump, a plugin they needed that does not exist in the ~80-app marketplace, a developer who refused to work with the Volusion API, or a gnawing worry since the 2020 bankruptcy. They are not naive — they know migration is work. They need someone to be straight with them about what that work actually is.

Secondary: B2B merchants who have outgrown Volusion's flat product catalog model and need tiered pricing, customer roles, or quote workflows that do not exist natively on the platform.

These readers are skim-first. The opening must earn the full read. They have already seen checklist articles — this piece must feel different from the first paragraph.

### Section 1: The walls you keep hitting (Act 1 — The before)

**H2 id:** `before`
**Heading (sentence case):** The walls you keep hitting on Volusion

**Key points to make:**

1. The sales cap structure is a pricing trap, not a growth feature. Personal plan caps at $50K/year, Professional at $100K, Business at $500K. When you cross the threshold, Volusion upgrades the account automatically. Merchants report no notification, bills jumping overnight, and in at least one documented case, revenue withheld during a dispute. This is not an edge case — it is the model.

2. The integration ceiling is architectural, not cosmetic. ~80 apps in the marketplace versus 60,000+ WordPress plugins and 1,100+ WooCommerce extensions. One Capterra reviewer called the API "an absolute disaster" with no agencies willing to build on it. If a tool you need does not have a native Volusion connector, you are stuck.

3. The platform has not kept pace. Multiple reviewers describe it as "a platform that hasn't been updated since the early 2000's." The 2020 bankruptcy — which put Volusion into Chapter 11 — was not just news; it was a signal about where investment was going. StoreLeads' 2026 data puts the platform at 3,526 active stores, down from 13,889 in Q1 2020. That is a 75% contraction.

4. For B2B merchants specifically: no native tiered pricing, no customer roles, no quote workflows. These are not nice-to-haves for a wholesale operation.

5. SEO has a structural ceiling. No native blog. URL canonicalization issues. Limited schema support. For a merchant running content-driven acquisition, this is a direct revenue constraint.

### Section 3: What migration actually involves (Act 3 — The honest migration)

**H2 id:** `migration`
**Heading (sentence case):** What the migration actually looks like

**Internal link placements:** Use the eCommerce migration checklist link and the WooCommerce complete guide link naturally within this section's prose.

**Key points to make (narrative, not a numbered checklist — the creator should write this as a walk-through, not a how-to list):**

1. Pre-migration: Export everything from Volusion Admin — products, customers, orders as CSV. Document every live URL because that list becomes your 301 redirect map. This step takes 1-3 days and is mostly unglamorous admin work. Do it anyway.

2. Environment setup: Choose a managed WordPress host (WP Engine, Kinsta, Cloudways, or SiteGround are reasonable choices). Install WordPress, install WooCommerce. Install an SEO plugin immediately — Yoast or RankMath — because WooCommerce has no native meta field management. Install a redirect manager plugin before anything goes live.

3. Data transfer: Run a test migration on a product subset using a tool like LitExtension or Cart2Cart (basic migrations start around $69). Check that product titles, prices, variants, customer records, and order history carry over cleanly. Fix field mapping mismatches before the full run.

4. What does NOT migrate automatically — be honest here:
   - Store design: must be rebuilt from scratch. Zero design carries over.
   - Payment gateways: reconfigured from scratch. New API keys.
   - Tax rules and shipping zones: must be rebuilt.
   - Complex custom fields without WooCommerce equivalents: dropped.

5. 301 redirects are the highest-risk step and the most commonly skipped. Volusion and WooCommerce use different URL structures by default. Every changed URL that does not have a 301 redirect is treated by Google as a new page. That means rankings drop — not because WooCommerce is bad for SEO, but because the redirect map was not complete. Set up monitoring in Google Search Console before and immediately after launch. Check for crawl errors at 4-6 weeks post-launch.

6. Timeline: 1-3 weeks for small-to-mid stores, 4-8 weeks for larger stores with custom features or large catalogs. Automated tools handle data transfer but not design, redirects, or SEO configuration.

7. Be honest about the complexity cost: plugin selection on WooCommerce requires judgment. Hosting is now the merchant's responsibility. A developer or agency relationship is more or less essential for anything beyond a basic store. This is not a "click install and done" migration.

### Section 5: People also ask

**H2 id:** `people-also-ask`
**Heading (sentence case):** People also ask

Four Q&As. Keep answers tight: 2-4 sentences each. Written in second person where possible.

**Q1: Is Volusion still in business?**
Yes. Volusion filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy in 2020 and continued operating. It emerged from bankruptcy protection and is still active, but the platform has contracted sharply — from roughly 13,889 active stores at its 2020 peak to 3,526 as of May 2026. The merchant base has declined by 75% in six years.

**Q2: How long does a Volusion to WooCommerce migration take?**
For a small to mid-size store, the migration typically takes 1-3 weeks. Larger stores with custom features, extensive catalogs, or complex integrations take 4-8 weeks. The data transfer itself is the faster part. Design rebuild, redirect mapping, and integration reconfiguration take the most time.

**Q3: Will I lose my SEO rankings if I migrate from Volusion to WooCommerce?**
Not if the 301 redirects are set up correctly. Every old Volusion URL that changes needs a redirect pointing to its new WooCommerce equivalent. If redirects are missing or incomplete, Google treats the new URLs as new pages and traffic drops. With a complete redirect map, rankings typically stabilize within a few months post-launch.

**Q4: How much does migrating from Volusion to WooCommerce cost?**
Automated data transfer tools start around $69 for basic migrations. That covers data only — not design, redirects, or configuration. A full migration involving a new WooCommerce theme, redirect mapping, plugin selection, and testing will typically require developer time. The total cost depends on store size, catalog complexity, and how much custom work the WooCommerce build requires.

### Section 7: FAQ accordion

**H2 id:** `faq`

Five Q&As using Template J. Covers specific operational questions a migrating merchant would ask:

1. **Can I run my Volusion store while the WooCommerce migration is in progress?**
   Yes. Build and test the WooCommerce store on a staging environment (or a temporary domain) before cutting DNS. Your Volusion store stays live until you make the switch, which minimises downtime to the DNS propagation window — typically a few hours.

2. **What happens to my order history and customer accounts when I migrate?**
   Both migrate via CSV export from Volusion and import into WooCommerce. Customer accounts, order history, and product records all carry over. Passwords do not migrate (password hashing differs between platforms) — customers will need to reset their passwords on first login.

3. **Do I need a developer to migrate from Volusion to WooCommerce?**
   For a basic store with a standard catalog and no custom integrations, a technical non-developer can use tools like LitExtension to handle data transfer and configure a WooCommerce theme. But 301 redirect mapping, SEO plugin configuration, and integration setup require technical judgment. Most merchants benefit from at least a short engagement with a WooCommerce developer for those phases.

4. **What about Volusion's payment processing — do I need to switch?**
   Volusion has its own payment gateway with maintenance fees ranging from 0.35% to 1.25% depending on plan. On WooCommerce you choose your own gateway. Most major providers — Stripe, PayPal, Square, Authorize.net — have official WooCommerce extensions. Payment gateway setup requires new API key configuration but is not technically complex.

5. **What data can I actually export from Volusion before I leave?**
   Via Admin > Data Management > Export, you can export: products (titles, descriptions, prices, images), customer accounts, order history, and category structure. Store settings, custom fields without WooCommerce equivalents, and design/theme assets do not transfer.

## 1. Thesis and angle

**Thesis:** Choosing between an RFQ form and a chat is a decision about the request, not a contest between tools: a form fits known part numbers and repeat buyers, a chat fits spec-heavy or unclear needs and after-hours questions, and both work best when they feed one shared quote queue that you measure with the same four timestamps.

**Why this angle:**
- Unclaimed lane. 43805 argues sequencing (store first, then chat). 42393 teaches plugin setup and the form field list. 41430 and 41511 sell chat and automation. Nobody on virtina.com, and no top-ranking page, treats form vs chat as a decision with honest limits, or defines how to measure quote response time.
- Competitors either sell forms (involve.me, Inchoo) or sell chat (Wonderchat, ChatSKU's own RFQ pages). Virtina is the neutral store-side voice: the back end both channels share (product data, ERP price and stock, routing, one queue) is the store owner's job, which is Virtina's territory.
- Differs from ChatSKU's angle on purpose ("forms are broken, chat fixes it", 15-practices listicle, 1.8% thesis). None of their stats are reused.
- Honest about evidence: no independent head-to-head test of form vs chat exists. The article says so and recommends a pilot on the reader's own traffic. That honesty is the credibility edge over stat-heavy competitors.

### 1. Why do RFQ forms lose buyers? (220 words, 2 H3s)
Locked first sentence: "RFQ forms lose buyers when they ask for details the buyer does not have yet, or ask for more than the price needs."
- H3 "What happens when the buyer has a spec but no part number?" Use the daytime buyer B. A required "part number" field blocks the one person who most needs help. Searching first fails too: link ecommerce site search (41808) as the step before the quote.
- H3 "Why do long forms stall?" Evidence, named in plain text, not hyperlinked: Nielsen Norman Group advises cutting any field whose data can be derived, collected later or dropped, and cut one of its own forms from 6 fields to 2. Baymard (June 2024) found the average checkout has 11.3 fields and most need about 8. State that this is consumer checkout, B2C evidence, and that neither source ties field count to RFQ conversion.
- Don't include: any conversion percentage, "x fields lowers conversion by y", the ChatSKU or vendor stats, "forms are broken".

### 3. When is chat better than a form? (300 words, 2 H3s)
Locked first sentence: "Chat is better than a form when the buyer cannot yet name the part, or asks outside your team's working hours."
- H3 "Which requests suit a chat?" Buyer B: spec, no part number. A chat can ask follow-up questions one at a time, point to matching items and gather quantity and date. Also after-hours questions. Say what a chat is bad at: firm custom pricing and unusual jobs belong with a person. Lead-time and stock questions get answered better when the store already holds that data (link backorder lead times, 43845, here or in section 4).
- H3 "What does a catalog chat add to the quote request?" **Template A, 120 to 180 words, one paragraph block, adapted not copied.** Order: the gap a form leaves, what ChatSKU is (use the approved short description in plain words), one concrete moment (buyer B describes the spec, the chat asks for quantity and timeline, the request reaches the quote queue complete), the honest limit, then the link line: "see how it works" -> `https://chatsku.com/rfq-automation-for-product-catalogs/` (`target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"`). Tone: natural next step, not an advert.
  - Features allowed (only these, from the verified page): conversational RFQs that collect product, quantity, timeline and contact details during the chat; 24/7 answers on specs, availability, alternatives and lead times; conversation logs and abandoned-quote analytics; customer-group and tiered pricing, RFQ workflows and quote management; import or sync via CSV, CRM or ERP; one-line script install on an existing site with no rebuild.
  - Honest limit (required): it works best on clean, structured product data, and catalog, pricing and permissions must be set up first. Firm pricing and complex custom jobs still go to a person. The chat hands off to the rep, it does not replace the rep.
  - Do not use: "12-field form", "8,000 sessions", "48-hour response", "$50K", "live in 24 hours", "under 30 minutes", any price or number, any claim that ChatSKU has a WooCommerce page or WooCommerce support. Never call ChatSKU a Virtina plugin.
- End with the decision line: use chat for spec-heavy or unclear needs and after-hours.

### 6. How do you measure quote response time? (270 words, 3 H3s)
Locked first sentence: "You measure quote response time by stamping the same four moments on every request, whichever channel it came from."
- H3 "Which four timestamps?" Request received, first acknowledgment, priced quote sent, quote accepted. Label as a plain way to define it, not an industry standard. Suggested measures with no benchmarks: median time to first reply, median time to priced quote, share of requests that arrive after hours, share needing a follow-up question for missing data.
- H3 "Calendar clock or business clock?" Zendesk keeps calendar-hour and business-hour values per ticket, and business-hour reporting needs a configured schedule and is not retroactive. Use it only to explain why an evening request looks fast on one clock and slow on the other. Name Zendesk in plain text, no link, say it is a helpdesk tool and the idea carries over.
- H3 "How do you tell a started quote from a finished one?" GA4 enhanced measurement `form_start` fires once per session on first interaction, `form_submit` fires on submit; started minus submitted is an abandonment signal. A chat has no HTML form, so it needs a custom "quote started" event: state this as inference, not fact. Site search: `view_search_results` fires when a results page loads, and GA4 does not log zero results by itself (state as inference).
- Don't include: any benchmark such as "respond within X minutes", HBR "42 hours / 7x / 60x", any stat from the rejected list, a link to ChatSKU's response-time post.

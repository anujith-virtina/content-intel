## 5. Per-entry verified facts

Legend: [V] = fetched from the marketplace's own page this run. [U] = NOT verified from a primary page. Company-reported = the company's own claim, label it that way in the post.

### 5.1 Alibaba.com [V]
- Source: https://www.alibaba.com/ (fetched 2026-11-02; page text was 103K characters, the last 3K not read).
- Serves: businesses sourcing products and the manufacturers, suppliers, exporters and importers who supply them.
- Model: open marketplace plus RFQ. Buyers post a request for quotation and get quotes from several suppliers. Sample and customisation services are listed.
- Company-reported claims on the page: calls itself the world's largest online B2B marketplace; 50M+ buyers; 34K+ verified manufacturers; 5K+ industries. The last two figures came from a summariser reading a long page. Re-read the exact wording and date before use. Use at most the buyer count, labelled company-reported.
- Ordering: every order is described as backed by secure payment, guaranteed delivery and a money-back guarantee, under the name Trade Assurance. Minimum order quantity is shown per listing (the page examples show 2 and 50) and the page states no platform-wide minimum.
- Fees: not stated on the page I fetched. Third-party figures ($3,499 and $6,999 per year) appear only in a competitor article. DO NOT use them.
- Region: global, sourcing mostly cross-border (inference from supplier categories; page does not give a region split).
- Honest limitation: ordering terms are set listing by listing (MOQ varies), so there is no uniform minimum or lead time to plan around. Verify each supplier.

### 5.2 Amazon Business [V]
- Sources: https://business.amazon.com/ (buyer side) and https://sell.amazon.com/programs/amazon-business (seller side), both fetched 2026-11-02.
- Serves: organisations buying supplies (education, healthcare, government, manufacturing, hospitality, nonprofits and others listed) and sellers who want to reach them.
- Model: open marketplace layered on Amazon retail. Buyer tools: centralised approval workflows, buying groups, user permissions, spend reports, quantity discounts and case packs, multiple payment methods.
- Seller tools stated: business-only prices and quantity discounts, case-pack and pallet listings, responding to quote requests, tax-exemption automation, fulfilment by self-ship or FBA.
- Fees: the seller page lists a Professional selling account at $39.99 per month plus selling fees, and says larger purchases may carry lower referral fees. No referral percentage stated on the page.
- Company-reported scale (seller page, undated on page): $35 billion annualised sales, 8 million organisations served globally excluding emerging markets, 97 Fortune 100 companies buying. Label as Amazon-reported and note the page gives no date.
- Region: buyer page lists sites in the US, India, UK, France, Germany, Spain, Italy, Canada, Japan, Mexico and Australia.
- Not on the buyer page: Net terms, pay-by-invoice, tax exemption. Do NOT claim Amazon Business offers net terms.
- Honest limitation: sellers pay Amazon selling fees and compete on Amazon's listing; the customer relationship sits with Amazon. (Link to 41015 for the deeper argument.)

### 5.3 IndiaMART [V]
- Source: https://www.indiamart.com/ (fetched 2026-11-02).
- Serves: buyers and suppliers, India focus.
- Model: open marketplace/directory. Buyers search or post a requirement. Sellers create a free listing by adding a business and at least three products or services (stated on the page). Verified sellers, payment protection and a lead feed called BuyLead are mentioned.
- Company-reported claim: describes itself as India's largest online B2B marketplace. The page's English counters showed zeros when fetched, so I could not read scale figures there. A Hindi section gave larger numbers; do NOT use them (cannot cross-check the language version).
- Fees: free basic listing is stated. Paid plans not stated on the page I fetched.
- Region: India, with an export-focused section mentioned. Top-cities lists.
- Honest limitation: built for India. A US buyer reaches suppliers through cross-border purchasing. The page does not describe US shipping or customs help.

### 5.4 ThomasNet [V]
- Source: https://business.thomasnet.com/about (fetched 2026-11-02; https://www.thomasnet.com/about/ 302-redirects there).
- Serves: industrial buyers (engineers, MRO and procurement professionals) and suppliers (manufacturers, distributors, service providers).
- Model: industrial sourcing directory and marketing platform. Buyers search, shortlist, send requests for information and get quotes from several suppliers in one step. Suppliers pay for visibility through a monthly advertising program where they pick keywords and a budget. There is no checkout.
- Company-reported claims: over 125 years in operation; profiles of more than 500,000 suppliers; over 80,000 industrial categories; 20 industry sectors.
- Region: North America.
- Honest limitation: it is a directory, not a place to place an order. Supplier ranking reflects advertising spend (page says suppliers pay for visibility via keyword campaigns). Verify the exact wording before using "ranking reflects spend" as a conclusion; say only that suppliers can pay for visibility.

## 7. "What this means for your store" angle (Virtina lane, not promotional)

Audience: manufacturer, distributor or wholesaler deciding between selling through marketplaces and running its own store.

Content, all derivable from verified facts above plus Virtina's own post 41015:
- Model decides fit. Open marketplaces (Alibaba, Amazon Business) bring buyers but the order lives on the marketplace. Curated wholesale (Ankorstore, Faire [U]) fits brands selling to independent retailers. Sourcing directories (ThomasNet, Made-in-China, IndiaMART) send an inquiry or RFQ and the order happens elsewhere. For directories, YOUR quote-to-order flow is the bottleneck, which is a store problem.
- Where the repeat order should live: marketplaces find the first order, a store holds negotiated pricing, reorders, net terms, account-specific catalogs, ERP sync. These are the themes Virtina owns (brand.md #1).
- Only verified fee fact to use: Amazon's Professional account line ($39.99 per month plus selling fees, seller page). Say other marketplaces' commissions vary and need checking on their own seller pages.
- Decision test (bullets are genuinely list-shaped here): who owns the buyer's contact details; whether negotiated prices are supported; whether net terms and POs are supported; ERP sync; how a suspension would affect revenue. Frame as questions to put to each marketplace.
- Keep claims modest. Do not say marketplaces are bad. Say they are acquisition, per 41015.
- Single soft CTA to /b2b-ecommerce-development/ and platform pages. No "our", no "check out".

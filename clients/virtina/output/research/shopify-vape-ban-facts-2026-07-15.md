# Fact verification: Shopify's ENDS/vape ban (June-July 2026)

Every claim below was checked against primary or reputable secondary sources on 2026-07-15. Verdict key: VERIFIED / PARTIALLY CORRECT / UNVERIFIED / WRONG.

## a. Did Shopify actually ban ENDS/vape stores? What exactly, effective when?

**VERIFIED, with correction to "full ban" framing.**

Shopify began sending individual merchant notices in **late June 2026** (multiple sources cite a notice dated **June 24, 2026**) stating it "no longer supports the sale of Electronic Nicotine Delivery Systems (ENDS)... due to changes in legal restrictions." The policy covers e-cigarettes, e-liquids, vaporizers, parts, and refills, **regardless of nicotine content and regardless of FDA authorization status**. Merchants were told to remove affected products by **July 7-8, 2026 UTC** (sources vary between July 7 and July 8) or risk product suspension or full store termination.

This was first widely reported by Reuters on June 23, 2026, and confirmed via merchant notices, not a single public press release. It is a full, platform-wide product ban, not a partial restriction, and it explicitly applies even to FDA-authorized SKUs, which several outlets flagged as unusual since regulators' original request targeted *illegal* vape sales specifically.

Third-party Shopify store-tracking tools (not primary sources, scraped estimates) put the total number of active Shopify stores in vape/vaping categories anywhere from **181 to 7,363**, depending on how narrowly "vape" is categorized. ## c. "Pressure from 25 State Attorneys General"

**VERIFIED.**

A bipartisan coalition of attorneys general from **25 states plus the District of Columbia, Puerto Rico, and the City of New York** sent a letter to Shopify in **November 2025**, co-led by California AG Rob Bonta and the City of New York. States: Arizona, California, Connecticut, Delaware, DC, Hawaii, Illinois, Indiana, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts, Michigan, Minnesota, New Mexico, New York, North Carolina, Ohio, Oregon, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island, Utah, Vermont, Washington, Wisconsin, Puerto Rico. ## d. "NDS Act" — name correction

**WRONG. There is no "NDS Act." The correct statute is the PACT Act.**

The relevant federal law is the **PACT Act** (Prevent All Cigarette Trafficking Act), originally enacted in 2010 to fight contraband cigarette trafficking. The **Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2021** (signed December 27, 2020, effective March 27, 2021) amended the PACT Act's definition of "cigarette" to include **Electronic Nicotine Delivery Systems (ENDS)** — this is sometimes informally called "the ENDS Amendment to the PACT Act." That is almost certainly the source of the "NDS Act" confusion: ENDS (the product category) got mistaken for a statute name. **Never use "NDS Act" in the draft.** Use "PACT Act" or, on first reference, "the PACT Act (Prevent All Cigarette Trafficking Act), extended to cover ENDS in 2021."

Under the PACT Act, any business selling/shipping ENDS in interstate commerce must:
- Register with the ATF and every state it ships into
- File monthly reports of ENDS shipments (product, quantity, recipient, carrier)
- Verify the age of the purchaser (21+) and require adult signature on delivery
- Keep invoices/records for four years
- Pay applicable state excise/tobacco taxes

Sources:
- [ATF: Prevent All Cigarette Trafficking (PACT) Act](https://www.atf.gov/alcohol-tobacco/prevent-all-cigarette-trafficking-pact-act)
- [Avalara: How the PACT Act impacts vaping, cannabis, and e-cigarettes](https://www.avalara.com/blog/en/north-america/2021/05/the-pact-act-now-includes-vaping-cannabis-and-other-e-cigarettes.html)

## e. PMTA — confirm and contextualize

**VERIFIED.**

PMTA = **Premarket Tobacco Product Application**, the FDA submission required under Section 910 of the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act before any ENDS product can be legally marketed in the US. It requires manufacturers to submit scientific data showing the product is "appropriate for the protection of public health." The approval bar is extremely high: of roughly 26 million PMTA submissions filed since October 2019, only a small number (widely cited range: **23-45 products**) have received a marketing authorization order as of 2026. [unverified — exact current count fluctuates; do not cite a precise number in the draft, say "a small fraction" or "fewer than 50 products nationally"]

This matters directly for the piece: it explains why almost every flavored disposable vape sold in the US is technically unauthorized, which is exactly the exposure regulators flagged and Shopify used to justify a blanket ban rather than a case-by-case one.

Source:
- [FDA: Premarket Tobacco Product Applications](https://www.fda.gov/tobacco-products/market-and-distribute-tobacco-product/premarket-tobacco-product-applications)

## f. Can you legally sell ENDS online in the US? What compliance applies?

**VERIFIED — yes, with significant compliance overhead, regardless of platform.**

Selling ENDS online is legal in the US only for products with FDA marketing authorization (a small list), sold in compliance with:
- **PACT Act registration and monthly reporting** (ATF + every destination state)
- **Age verification** (21+) at purchase and **adult signature required at delivery**
- **Shipping carrier restrictions**: USPS banned ENDS shipments in October 2021; UPS, FedEx, and DHL followed with their own voluntary bans on consumer (B2C) vapor shipments. B2B/wholesale shipping between registered, ATF-compliant businesses remains possible through specialized common carriers, not standard USPS/UPS/FedEx/DHL consumer service.
- **State and local flavor bans / product bans**: many states and cities separately restrict or ban flavored ENDS or ENDS sales entirely, independent of federal rules.
- **PMTA/FDA authorization status** of the specific SKU

None of this compliance burden changes based on which ecommerce platform a merchant uses — it is a product-category obligation, not a Shopify-specific one. This is a key point for the FAQ: moving to WooCommerce does not remove compliance requirements, it removes platform-level deplatforming risk.

## g. Why are ENDS merchants "high-risk" to payment processors?

**VERIFIED.**

Payment processors and card networks classify ENDS/vape merchants as high-risk because of:
- **Age-restricted product liability** — any lapse in age verification exposes the processor to regulatory and reputational risk
- **Elevated chargeback rates** — combination of regulatory volatility, product-satisfaction disputes, and fraud
- **Regulatory instability** — rules change state by state with little notice, raising compliance risk for the processor, not just the merchant
- **Reputational risk to banks/processors** — issuing banks avoid categories associated with underage sales or illegal interstate shipping

Mainstream processors (Stripe, PayPal, Square) explicitly prohibit vape transactions in their acceptable-use policies. This means even a merchant who successfully migrates off Shopify still cannot use default payment options — they need a **high-risk merchant account** through an acquiring bank or payment gateway that specifically underwrites regulated/age-restricted categories. High-risk accounts typically carry higher processing rates, reserve requirements, and more documentation (processing history, business plan, compliance procedures) during underwriting. This is true on WooCommerce, BigCommerce, or any platform — it is a payment-network-level restriction, not a storefront-platform restriction. No specific processor names should be endorsed in the draft (per brand rules); describe the category (high-risk merchant account, high-risk payment gateway) generically.

Sources:
- [PayCompass: Vape Shop Credit Card Processing Guide](https://paycompass.com/blog/vape-shop-credit-card-processing-guide/)
- General synthesis across multiple high-risk payments industry sources (paymentnerds.com, allaypay.com, vectorpayments.com) — none individually authoritative enough to single-source; treat as industry-consensus, not a unique citable claim.

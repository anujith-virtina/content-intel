# Competitor analysis — Square hemp and CBD cutoff

## Method

Three searches run 2026-09-11, as required by MUST-FOLLOW section 4c:

1. `Square CBD hemp ban October 15 2026`
2. `hemp Section 781 November 12 2026 delay House vote`
3. `"CBD payment processor" WooCommerce high risk merchant account`

Four top-ranking pages were fetched in full and assessed. SERP positions are as returned on the date of
the search and will drift. Two pages could not be fetched (HTTP 403) and are documented honestly at the
end rather than guessed at.

---

## THE EXPLOITABLE BIAS — read this before the individual entries

> **Every page ranking for this topic is published by someone selling the thing the reader is about to
> buy.** Payment processors, a cannabis POS vendor, and a hemp law firm. Four out of four fetched
> results. Zero neutral publishers in the fetched set.

This is not a complaint, it is the opening. It produces a specific and predictable blind spot:

**None of them can tell a shop owner "you may not need a new store, you may need a different product
line."** A processor that says that loses the sale. A POS vendor that says that loses the sale. A law
firm that says it plainly, without a consultation, loses the lead.

Virtina can say it, because Virtina is not selling the merchant account. Virtina sells the store build.
That single honest concession is the most useful sentence available on this topic and it is
structurally unbuyable by every competitor in the SERP.

The second exploitable gap is narrower and sharper: **not one fetched page mentions Square Loans.**
Square's own help centre documents that closing the business puts an outstanding loan in default,
permits a demand for the full balance, and permits a debit of the linked bank account. It is sitting in
public on squareup.com and the entire SERP walked past it.

The third: **three of the four are already factually stale on the effective date**, because the delay
was signed on September 2, 2026.

## 1. Evolve Payment — the strongest competitor

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **SERP position** | ~4 for "Square CBD hemp ban October 15 2026" |
| **URL** | https://www.evolvepayment.com/blog/square-is-cutting-off-hemp-and-cbd-merchants-heres-what-actually-changed-and-what-operators-should-do-before-october-15/ |
| **Title** | "Square Is Cutting Off Hemp and CBD Merchants. Here's What Actually Changed, and What Operators Should Do Before October 15." |
| **Domain** | evolvepayment.com — merchant services provider, St. Paul, Minnesota. Sells high-risk payment processing, including CBD and cannabis. |
| **Est. word count** | ~3,500 |
| **Structure** | What the 2018 Farm Bill did → what Section 781 changes → why this is a banking problem → why processors are moving before Congress finishes → timeline table → what operators should do in 60 days → "where we come at this from" → sources |

This is the best page in the set. It genuinely explains Section 781, cites its sources, and carries a
timeline table. Treat it as the bar to clear, not as filler.

### Three specific weaknesses

1. **It never mentions Square Loans.** The single highest-stakes financial consequence of the account
   closing is absent from a 3,500-word article published by a payments company. For a merchant carrying
   a balance, this is the most expensive omission on the page.
2. **It treats December 11 as hypothetical.** It lists December 11, 2026 as a "potential delayed
   effective date" alongside November 12. It was written before the House vote on September 1 and the
   signature on September 2. It is now stale on the one fact the whole story turns on.
3. **No data-portability content at all.** Nothing on the item library export, the transactions export,
   the customer directory, or the card-on-file export that takes up to two weeks and requires a phone
   call. A reader finishes it knowing the law and still not knowing whether they keep their customers.

### How Virtina outperforms

1. Square Loan mechanics quoted from Square's own help centre: default on closure, possible demand for
   the full balance, possible debit of the linked bank account, and the alternative arrangement that
   exists but must be requested in writing before the account closes.
2. The December 11 date stated as **settled law**, sourced to the enrolled text of H.R. 6500, Division
   B, Section 2019 on govinfo.gov, with the November 12 synthetic-cannabinoid carve-out explained
   separately. Evolve has the question; Virtina has the answer.
3. A full what-comes-with-you and what-does-not list, including the card-on-file export timing that
   reverses the order most merchants assume they can work in.

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **SERP position** | ~6 for "Square CBD hemp ban October 15 2026"; top 5 for the Section 781 query |
| **URL** | https://www.hemplawgroup.com/news/federal-hemp-ban-2026-where-things-stand |
| **Title** | "Federal Hemp Ban 2026: Where Things Stand" |
| **Domain** | hemplawgroup.com — law firm specialising in hemp compliance. Lead magnet for consultations. |
| **Est. word count** | ~4,500 |
| **Structure** | Current status as of Sept 3 → "the short version" → what changed federally in 2025 → when it takes effect → which products are affected → what is still undefined → what Congress is doing → the 2026 Farm Bill → Tennessee TABC interaction → what it means for businesses and for buyers → 12-question FAQ |

The only fetched page that is **correct on December 11**. Updated September 3, one day after signature.
On pure legal accuracy it beats everything else in the SERP.

### Three specific weaknesses

1. **Almost no payments content.** It mentions Square's October 15 deadline in passing and stops.
   A merchant reading it learns the law and learns nothing about what happens to their money, their
   payouts, their loan, or their processor.
2. **Zero ecommerce platform content.** No discussion of storefronts, POS, sales channels, or what
   rebuilding a store actually involves. The reader is left with a legal picture and no next action
   that involves their actual business operations.
3. **Heavy Tennessee bias.** A full section on Tennessee TABC rules, other states covered minimally.
   A shop owner in Oregon or Texas gets a page that is 4,500 words long and partly about somewhere else.

### How Virtina outperforms

1. The law is treated as the **cause**, not the subject. Virtina answers "what does this do to my
   business this month", which is the question a shop owner actually types.
2. Store, POS and processor are covered as three separable problems with three different timelines,
   and the post is explicit that solving one does not solve the others.
3. No state-by-state pretence. Virtina states that state rules vary, tells the reader to check their own
   state agency, and does not publish a list it cannot verify from a government source. Shorter and more
   honest than a partial list weighted to one state.

## 3. Vector Payments

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **SERP position** | ~3 for "Square CBD hemp ban October 15 2026" |
| **URL** | https://www.vectorpayments.com/square-is-shutting-down-cbd-and-hemp-merchant-accounts-by-november-5-2026/ |
| **Title** | "Square Is Shutting Down CBD and Hemp Merchant Accounts by November 5, 2026" |
| **Domain** | vectorpayments.com — high-risk payment processor. Positions itself explicitly as the Square alternative. |
| **Est. word count** | ~2,500 to 3,000 |
| **Structure** | Deadline announcement → quick answer → FAQ-style sections → comparison table → CTA |
| **Published** | August 18, 2026 |

Well structured for search, correct on both Square dates, and heavily conversion-oriented. It also runs
a companion page, "How to Switch CBD Payment Processors Before Nov 5", which is the same funnel.

### Three specific weaknesses

1. **Thin on the law.** It gestures at the 2018 Farm Bill and notes federal timelines have "shifted at
   least once" without explaining the total-THC standard, the 0.4 mg per container cap, or the
   synthetic-cannabinoid split. A reader cannot work out whether their own products survive.
2. **The comparison table compares Vector to Square.** It is a sales asset in the shape of an
   information asset. No neutral evaluation criteria, no mention of what any alternative costs in time.
3. **No data or lending content.** Nothing on exports, saved cards, payouts, reserves, or loans. Like
   Evolve, it skips the money entirely while being published by a money company.

### How Virtina outperforms

1. Names the actual test a merchant can apply to their own shelf: total THC including THCA at 0.3% dry
   weight for the plant, and 0.4 milligrams of total THC **per container** for the finished product,
   where a container is the innermost retail packaging. That is a check the reader can run tonight.
2. Names no specific processors at all. Describes the category and what to ask for. The article does not
   date itself and does not hand a competitor credibility.
3. Covers the three things Vector omits: what exports cleanly, what does not, and what the loan does.

Ranking first on the most commercially valuable query of the three, on content that predates the entire
story.

### Three specific weaknesses

1. **It does not mention the 2026 hemp law or Square's ban at all.** It references only the 2018 Farm
   Bill. It is ranking first for a WooCommerce CBD payments query while being silent on the two events
   that changed WooCommerce CBD payments. This is the clearest content gap in the whole SERP.
2. **No pricing, no fee structure, no processor comparison.** It tells the reader they need a high-risk
   account and routes them to an application form. The decision framework is "call us".
3. **No state-by-state content, no compliance content, no COA content.** A merchant following this page
   could build a WooCommerce store and still be selling something that is not hemp in December.

### How Virtina outperforms

1. The post is anchored to the two events PaymentCloud ignores, with primary sources for both.
2. It gives the reader a sequence they can follow without a phone call: sort the catalog, choose the
   processor, rescue the saved cards, rebuild the store. In that order, with the reasons for the order.
3. It covers the compliance basics in plain language: what a certificate of analysis is and why the new
   measurement changes what it has to show, why FDA's position means health claims stay off the label,
   age verification, and why state rules have to be checked locally.

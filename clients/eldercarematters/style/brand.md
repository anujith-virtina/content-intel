# Brand — eldercarematters

## Positioning

ElderCareMatters.com is a national directory of elder care resources for American families. Operated by ElderCareMatters, LLC. The site's own claims, taken from the live homepage on 2026-09-01:

- "America's National Directory of Elder Care Resources" (site tagline in the WordPress REST description)
- "America's oldest and most respected Elder Care Directory"
- "Trusted Since 2002", currently expressed as "24 Amazing Years"
- "500+ Verified Providers"
- 50+ provider categories

**Verify these numbers against the live homepage before repeating them in a post.** The provider count and the years figure both change, and a stale number in an evergreen article ages badly.

## Business model

Two revenue sides:

- Providers pay for directory listings and featured placements ("List Your Company")
- Families use the directory free

The blog exists to bring families to the directory. Content should end by pointing a reader toward the right category of provider, not toward a sales page.

## Provider categories the directory covers

Home care, assisted living, memory care and Alzheimer's care, skilled nursing, hospice and palliative care, elder law, Medicaid planning, asset protection, care management, financial planning, adult day care, aging in place, advance directives, veterans benefits. The live category list runs to thousands of granular terms, so always pull the real category ID from the API rather than guessing.

## The editorial line

The site's value is that it is old, national, and does not push one care type. Content must stay genuinely neutral between care options. A post comparing home care and assisted living must be usable by a reader who ends up choosing either one, because the directory lists both.

## Things never to say

- Never recommend a specific named provider, community, or firm. The directory is the recommendation.
- Never imply a listing is an endorsement or that "verified" means vetted for quality, unless the client confirms exactly what verification involves.
- Never state that Medicare pays for long-term custodial care. It does not, and this is the site's most important factual guardrail.
- Never give a Medicaid eligibility figure, look-back period, or asset limit without a dated citation, because these vary by state and change annually.
- Never quote a national average cost of care without naming the source and the year.
- Never use fear as a call to action.
- Never suggest a family is neglecting their parent by choosing a cheaper option.

## Competitors: do not cite or link

Directories and lead-generation sites in the same category: A Place for Mom, Caring.com, SeniorAdvisor, Senior Living, AgingCare, Assisted Living Locators, Care.com.

## Acceptable to cite and link

Government and nonprofit primary sources only: Medicare.gov, Medicaid.gov, CMS, the National Institute on Aging, the Administration for Community Living and its Eldercare Locator, the Alzheimer's Association, the VA, AARP, the Family Caregiver Alliance.

## Themes ElderCareMatters can own

1. Deciding between care types when the decision is urgent
2. What Medicare and Medicaid actually cover, and what they do not
3. Questions to ask on a tour or an intake call
4. Paying for care: sources of money families overlook, including VA Aid and Attendance
5. Caring for a parent from another state
6. Sibling disagreement and family decision-making
7. Recognising when a care level needs to change

## Themes to avoid

- Anything that reads as medical guidance about a condition
- Anything that reads as legal advice about a specific family's situation
- Retirement lifestyle content unrelated to care decisions
- Provider marketing advice on the family-facing blog

## Call to action

Send the reader into the directory for the relevant provider category, or to the Eldercare Locator for public help. Do not gate anything behind a form.

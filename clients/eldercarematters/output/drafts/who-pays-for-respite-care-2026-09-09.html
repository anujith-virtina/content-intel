Introduction

You have found respite care that would help, and now you need to know who pays for it. That question has more answers than most families are told.

If you are still deciding what kind of break you need, our guide to respite care basics covers the forms it takes. This post is about the money.

Most families start by paying out of pocket. An aide at home has a national median rate of $35 an hour. That figure is from the CareScout 2025 Cost of Care Survey, released in March 2026.

The payers below are in the order most families should try them, not alphabetical order. The two doors families miss most often are at the top.

No single payer covers respite for every family, so work down this list in order.

What Is the Free Respite Program Most Families Never Hear About?

It is the National Family Caregiver Support Program, funded under Title III-E of the Older Americans Act. You reach it through your Area Agency on Aging.

The Administration for Community Living funds it through the states, and respite is one of five service categories. The others are information, access help, counseling, training, and supplemental services.

Who Qualifies for This Help

  • Caring for someone aged 60 or over
  • Caring for someone of any age living with Alzheimer's or a related dementia
  • A grandparent aged 55 or over raising a grandchild
  • A relative aged 55 or over caring for an adult child with a disability

How to Reach Your Area Agency on Aging

Call the Eldercare Locator on 1-800-677-1116, a free public service of the Administration for Community Living. That number also reaches your Aging and Disability Resource Center, or ADRC.

This funding is not means tested the way Medicaid is, but availability, waiting times, and any voluntary contribution vary by agency.

Can Medicare Pay for Respite If Your Parent Has Dementia?

Yes, through Medicare's GUIDE Model, if your parent has a dementia diagnosis and their doctor takes part. GUIDE stands for Guiding an Improved Dementia Experience.

It is a Medicare dementia care model that includes up to $2,500 a year for respite. AARP reported that figure, updated 6 October 2025.

The benefit can pay for respite at home, at an adult day center, or in a round-the-clock setting. Our adult day care guide explains how day programs work.

Eligibility is narrow. Your parent needs a dementia diagnosis and enrollment in Original Medicare Parts A and B.

Your parent cannot be on hospice or in a long-term nursing home, and Medicare Advantage enrollees are excluded.

Here is the part most articles miss. You cannot sign your parent up for GUIDE yourself. Call your parent's primary care provider, ask whether the practice takes part, then ask about respite.

Participants also get a named care navigator, caregiver training, and a 24-hour support line. The Alzheimer's Association says these carry no charge to eligible participants.

Our directory also lists memory care communities by state.

Does Medicare Cover Respite Care Otherwise?

No. Outside the GUIDE Model, Original Medicare does not pay for respite, with one narrow exception under the hospice benefit. NCOA said so on 12 December 2024.

It will not pay an aide to sit with your parent so you can sleep, or for an adult day program.

The exception applies once your parent has elected the Medicare hospice benefit, which includes short-term inpatient respite.

CMS, on a page dated 2 July 2026, describes up to five consecutive days at a time in an approved inpatient setting. That is five days per stay, not five days a year.

Coinsurance is 5% of the Medicare-approved amount, capped at that year's inpatient hospital deductible. Our explainer on how hospice care works covers electing hospice.

How Does Medicaid Pay for Respite Care?

Medicaid pays for respite mainly through home and community based services. The rules are set state by state.

Waivers and State Plan Services

Respite is one of the services a state may cover under a Medicaid 1915(c) waiver, shortened to HCBS waiver.

Some states use the Medicaid state plan instead of a waiver, so ask your state Medicaid office about both routes.

Can a Family Member Be Paid?

Sometimes, yes. Self-directed programs let a Medicaid-eligible person control a budget and choose their own workers.

Some states require that worker to enroll as a Medicaid provider first. ARCH reports most states offer some self-direction, with rules that differ everywhere.

Eligibility is a legal and financial question that varies by state. Speak to an elder law attorney before you move any money.

What Respite Help Does the VA Offer?

The VA runs three routes to respite.

The Standard VA Respite Benefit

Enrolled veterans who meet clinical criteria can use it at home, in adult day programs, or in an inpatient setting. The VA's fact sheet describes it as ordinarily no more than 30 days a year, and a copay may apply.

PCAFC, for Veterans With a 70% Rating or Higher

The Program of Comprehensive Assistance for Family Caregivers is the larger benefit. VA.gov requires a rating of 70% or higher and VA health care enrollment.

The veteran must also hold a discharge or medical discharge date, and need at least six months of continuous in-person care. The caregiver must be 18 or over, and related to the veteran or living with them.

VA.gov lists a monthly stipend and at least 30 days of respite a year for Primary Family Caregivers. Apply with the veteran, online, by mail on VA Form 10-10CG, or at a VA medical center.

PGCSS, the Program With No Application Form

The Program of General Caregiver Support Services covers anyone giving personal care to an enrolled veteran, related or not. It is the route when a family does not clear 70%.

Services include training, peer support, and respite, and there is no application form. Contact your local Caregiver Support Coordinator, complete an intake, and the veteran confirms you.

Under Veteran-Directed Care the veteran gets a budget and may hire a family member or neighbor. Ask the VA social worker which route fits, and see our post on veterans benefits explained.

Will Long-Term Care Insurance Pay for Respite?

Often yes, if the policy names respite as a covered service. The money starts only after two gates.

Gate one is the benefit trigger. NCOA, in guidance dated 21 August 2026, says policies pay out on cognitive impairment or trouble with two or more activities of daily living.

Those activities, or ADLs, include bathing, dressing, eating, toileting, and moving around.

Gate two is the elimination period, a wait of roughly one to three months during which your family pays.

A family buying four hours a week and never filing a claim never starts that clock.

Tell the insurer early and in writing, and confirm what the policy pays for respite and for how many days.

What If None of These Cover Your Family?

A fourth tier exists, and it is where most families land.

Some states run Lifespan Respite programs outside Medicaid, offering vouchers, grants, or stipends to families who do not qualify elsewhere. Not every state has one.

Reach yours through your state respite coalition or your ADRC. The ARCH National Respite Network and Family Caregiver Alliance map state options.

Ask about sliding fee scales and scholarships, which many centers do not publish. Local Alzheimer's Association chapters run caregiver programs, and NCOA reported on 14 June 2024 that HFC serves dementia caregivers.

Military families outside the VA can ask about TRICARE's Extended Care Health Option and the Exceptional Family Member Program.

Paying Privately, and How to Lower the Rate

Most respite is bought directly from home care providers. Ask about minimum visit lengths, cancellation terms, and direct billing.

Can Your Employer Help?

An employer can give you time, but almost never money.

The Family and Medical Leave Act gives eligible workers 12 weeks of unpaid, job-protected leave in a 12-month period, with health insurance continuing. AARP set out the terms on 5 September 2024.

Leave can be taken intermittently or as a reduced schedule.

Eligibility means 12 months with the employer, 1,250 hours in the past year, and 50 or more employees within 75 miles. Parents, spouses, and children are covered, but in-laws, siblings, and grandparents generally are not.

FMLA buys time, not care. Ask HR about backup adult care or a dependent care account.

Questions to Ask on the Funding Call

Take these to whichever call you make first.

  1. Does my Area Agency on Aging fund respite now, and is there a wait?
  2. How long is the wait, and what helps meanwhile?
  3. Does my parent's practice take part in Medicare's GUIDE Model?
  4. Does our state cover respite by waiver, state plan, or both?
  5. Can we self-direct the budget and choose our own worker?
  6. Which VA respite programs qualify, and will a copay apply?
  7. Does the elimination period apply to respite, and for how many days a year?
  8. Do you bill this funder directly, or do I claim it back?

Gather These Before You Call

Every call goes faster with these in front of you.

  • The Medicare card, and whether it is Original or Advantage
  • Any dementia diagnosis, who made it, and the date
  • Your parent's ZIP code, because programs are routed locally
  • Whether your parent has Medicaid, and in which state
  • The veteran's disability rating and VA medical center
  • The long-term care policy number and the insurer's number
  • The hours you need covered, as days and times
  • A notebook for who you spoke to, and when

People Also Ask

Is Respite Care Ever Free?

Some of it is. Respite funded under the Older Americans Act, GUIDE services, and volunteer programs can carry no charge.

Does Medicare Advantage Cover Respite Care?

Some plans do. NCOA named in-home respite and adult day services among Advantage supplemental benefits on 12 December 2024. Check the plan's evidence of coverage.

Is There a Waiting List for State-Funded Respite Care?

Often, yes. Waivers cap how many people they serve, and agency budgets are finite. Ask how long the list is.

What Does the GUIDE Model Provide Besides Respite?

A named care navigator, caregiver training, and a 24-hour support line. The Alzheimer's Association states these carry no charge to eligible participants.

Conclusion

Usually more than one payer pays, and rarely the one families expect. Work the list in order, because the first call is free.

That call is the Eldercare Locator on 1-800-677-1116, which reaches your Area Agency on Aging. If your parent has dementia, the next call is to their doctor, about GUIDE.

If you cannot work out which door applies, care managers assess the situation and put a plan in writing.

The funding is not hidden. The mechanics are, and now you have them.

Frequently Asked Questions

What If My Parent's VA Disability Rating Is Under 70%?

PCAFC is out of reach, but the general VA respite benefit and PGCSS are not. PGCSS needs no rating and no application form.

What If My Parent's Doctor Does Not Take Part in GUIDE?

Ask whether another practice nearby does. AARP reported on 6 October 2025 that coverage is wide but not complete.

Does PACE Cover Respite Care?

PACE is a combined Medicare and Medicaid program for older adults who qualify for nursing home level care but want to stay home. Its day center gives the caregiver regular daytime hours. Ask your local program about formal respite.

Will Paying for Respite Affect My Parent's Medicaid Eligibility?

Eligibility rules vary by state and change. Anything touching your parent's assets or income belongs with a licensed elder law attorney.

Is Respite Care Tax Deductible?

That is a question for a tax professional.

Where Can I Find Respite Providers Near Me?

The ARCH National Respite Locator is a free national database, though ARCH says it is not complete and does not endorse providers. The Eldercare Locator, on 1-800-677-1116, also lists local options.
